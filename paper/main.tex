\begin{abstract}
Diffusion transformers trained near one megapixel fail at $4096^2$: attention meets token counts and position offsets it never saw, at 256 times the cost. Training-free fixes set how far each head looks by hand or per target size, since at the target full attention fails. At the training resolution, where full attention is still valid, reach is a property of the head: after the first steps, the head explains \hlVarHead\% of the variance in how much attention stays near the query, and the noise level \hlVarStep\%. We introduce Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}), which gives each head its own mix of a native-size window and a native-size grid over the whole image. Reach emulation shrinks both views at $1024^2$ to the target's image-to-window ratio, so the model's own full attention fits each mix in closed form, once for every size. In HRDiT's three-stage schedule, \sca{} makes a $4096^2$ FLUX.1-dev image in \hlSecProg\,s, $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA and $\hlSpeedProgHrdit\times$ faster than HRDiT (Figure~\ref{fig:speed}). It beats AReA on all ten per-image metrics at three seeds and on HRGen-1K and matches HRDiT's preference scores. A single-pass mode \ifanynewmodel runs in \hlSecSca\,s, $\hlSpeedArea\times$ faster than AReA, and \sca{} also runs on \newmodelList.\else keeps more fine detail and runs in \hlSecSca\,s, $\hlSpeedArea\times$ faster than AReA.\fi{} Code: \codeurl.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Diffusion transformers~\citep{peebles2022dit} such as FLUX.1~\citep{flux2024} and Stable Diffusion~3~\citep{esser2024sd3} are trained near one megapixel. At $4096\times4096$ the image has 16 times as many tokens, full attention costs about 256 times as much, and every layer meets token counts and position offsets it never saw in training. Full attention then returns stripes or a flat field (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). Fixes that restrict attention must decide how far each head looks, and at the target nothing can check that decision, because full attention there is the failure. We ask whether the decision can be measured where full attention is still correct, at the training resolution.

At the training resolution, each query's attention splits exactly into a near part around the query and a far part over the rest of the image, weighted by the near share $m_i$ (Figure~\ref{fig:focus}a). How much a head keeps near is its \emph{reach}, and heads differ sharply. At one query, head~\hlFocusHeadNear{} of block~\hlFocusBlock{} keeps $m_i=\hlFocusNearM$ of its image attention in a window 16 tokens wide, while head~\hlFocusHeadFar{} keeps $\hlFocusFarM$ and looks at the other sheep. Over 32 prompts, after the first 8 steps, the head explains \hlVarHead\% of the variance in the share of attention a query keeps within 16 tokens, and the noise level \hlVarStep\% (Figure~\ref{fig:focus}c).

Training-free fixes edit the positional encoding~\citep{zhuo2024lumina,issachar2025dype,zhao2025ultraimage,rajabi2026sega} or restrict the keys each query sees~\citep{zhang2023hidiffusion,ma2025resdit,xue2026hrdit,rajabi2026area}. AReA~\citep{rajabi2026area} finds that attention narrows during denoising, averaged over heads, and gives every head one schedule: pooled keys for 8 of 28 steps, then windows that shrink to native size. HRDiT searches per-head scopes for each target size, and UltraImage types heads at run time~\citep{xue2026hrdit,zhao2025ultraimage}.

We introduce Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}), which keeps both views at native size at any resolution (Figure~\ref{fig:method}a). The near view is an exact native-size window around the query, and the far view a native-size grid of block-centre keys over the whole image. One weight per head mixes them, so a near-reaching head reads the window and a far-reaching head the grid (Figure~\ref{fig:focus}a, right). We fit the weights by \emph{reach emulation}. At $1024^2$, shrinking the window and the grid by the target's image-to-window ratio $r$ keeps the target's proportions on an image where full attention is still correct (Figure~\ref{fig:focus}b). The model's own full attention then scores both views, and the best weight has a closed form. One emulation pass fills the \emph{reach table}, one weight per block, head, noise level, and ratio, which serves every target size. We call this scale covariance: the weight depends on the head and the image-to-window ratio, not on the absolute size.

\sca{} replaces only the attention, so it runs inside any sampler. Our main configuration keeps HRDiT's released three-stage schedule~\citep{xue2026hrdit}, which sets the layout at $1024^2$ and refines it at $2048^2$ and $4096^2$, and swaps HRDiT's four attention changes for \sca{}. We call it progressive \sca{}; the schedule is HRDiT's (Section~\ref{sec:progressive}). It makes a $4096^2$ image in \hlSecProg\,s on one GH200, $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA, $\hlSpeedProgHrdit\times$ faster than HRDiT, and $\hlSpeedProgUltraCamp\times$ faster than UltraImage in campaign time (Figure~\ref{fig:speed}). A single-pass mode samples at the target in 28 steps: it opens with the global view for 8 steps, as AReA does, then shrinks a window to native size over four steps. The opening, the window sizes, and 1,024 bridge tokens that show each window the whole image are set by hand; only the weights are fitted (Table~\ref{tab:handset}).

The fitted weights are stable across disjoint prompts (correlation 0.99), track each head's attention mass within 16 tokens ($\rho=0.77$), and transfer across ratios and resolutions (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). At $4096^2$ on FLUX.1-dev, progressive \sca{} beats AReA on all ten per-image metrics for each of three seeds and on HRGen-1K; at seed 0 ImageReward rises from \hlAreaIrAbs{} to \hlProgIrAbs. On set-level scores it is level with AReA on FID, and its patch FID is higher at two of three seeds. It matches HRDiT on ImageReward, HPSv2.1, and PickScore in \hlProgHrditPct\% of HRDiT's time, and trails it on KID and patch FID. Against single-pass \sca{} it gains preference and loses fine detail, with MUSIQ on $1024^2$ crops $\hlProgScaMusiqc$ and patch FID $\hlProgScaFidP$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item We show that the reach of attention heads, measured at the training resolution, varies far more between heads than between denoising steps on FLUX.1-dev, FLUX.1-Krea-dev, and FLUX.2-dev, and we fit each head's mix once at $1024^2$, in closed form, by reach emulation (Sections~\ref{sec:emulation} and~\ref{sec:analysis}).
  \item We show that at $4096^2$ progressive \sca{} is $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA, beats it on all ten per-image metrics for three seeds, and matches HRDiT in less than half its time (Section~\ref{sec:main}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Scale-Covariant Attention}
\label{sec:method}
Each head gets two views of the image that keep the training geometry, a near view and a far view, and one weight, its reach, for how much of its output comes from each. Only the weight is measured: it is fitted at $1024^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:emulation}) and read at the target's image-to-window ratio (Section~\ref{sec:sched}).

\subsection{Setting and notation}
\label{sec:setting}
A multimodal diffusion transformer such as FLUX.1 concatenates $T$ text tokens $\Tset$ with $N=HW$ image tokens on the grid $\Gset=[H]\times[W]$, where $[n]=\{0,\dots,n-1\}$. Each block applies joint attention (57 blocks of 24 heads in FLUX.1). It is trained at about $1024^2$ pixels, a native grid of $n_0\times n_0$ tokens with $n_0=64$. Rotary embeddings~\citep{su2021roformer} give token $j$ a position $p_j\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ (FLUX.1 puts all text tokens at $0$) and a rotation $R_p$ with $R_p^\top R_{p'}=R_{p'-p}$. For one head, a pre-rotation query $q$ placed at $u$, and a set $\Kset$ of (key, value, key position) triples,
\begin{equation}
  \Attn(q,u;\Kset) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{(k,v,u')\in\Kset} \operatorname{softmax}_{\Kset}\!\big(q^\top R_{u'-u}\,k/\sqrt d\big)\; v ,
  \label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the head dimension. Full attention is $\Ofull_i=\Attn\big(q_i,p_i;\{(k_j,v_j,p_j)\}_{j\in\Tset\cup\Gset}\big)$. The target grid is $H=s_H n_0$, $W=s_W n_0$ with integer factors ($s$ when equal), so full attention meets offsets $s$ times the trained range. The latent at noise level $\sigma$ is $x_\sigma=(1-\sigma)x_0+\sigma\epsilon$. Splitting the keys into near and far sets gives exactly $\Ofull_i=m_iO^{\mathrm{near}}_i+(1-m_i)O^{\mathrm{far}}_i$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:split}), where each $O$ attends to its own set and $m_i$ is the near set's softmax mass (Figure~\ref{fig:focus}a). Every head thus mixes a near and a far view per query; \sca{} replaces each view with an operator that keeps the training geometry and mixes the two with one weight per head.

\subsection{Near view and far view}
\label{sec:branches}

\paragraph{Near view: the local operator.} Image queries form tiles of $t\times t$ tokens ($t=8$); query $i$ lies in tile $\tau=\lfloor p_i/t\rfloor$. The window $\Win_w(i)$ of tile $\tau$ has side $w$, at most the grid side. It starts at $a(\tau)=\mbox{$\clip\big(t\tau+\lfloor t/2\rfloor-\lfloor w/2\rfloor,0,H-w\big)$}$ per axis, centred on the tile and clamped to the grid. Its keys are the text tokens, a bridge set $\mathcal{B}$ of image tokens, and the window, all at their original positions:
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i(w)=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_j,v_j,p_j)\}_{j\in\mathcal{B}\cup\Win_w(i)},\qquad
  \Oloc_i(w)=\Attn\big(q_i,\,p_i;\,\Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i(w)\big).
  \label{eq:loc}
\end{equation}
The bridge takes, in each cell $c$ of an $8\times8$ partition of the grid, the 16 tokens whose pre-rotation keys lie farthest from the mean image key $\bar k^{h}$, summed over heads $h$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{B}=\textstyle\bigcup_{c}\operatorname{top}_{16}\big\{\sum_{h}\|k^{h}_j-\bar k^{h}\|^2 : j\in c\big\}.
  \label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
It is recomputed at every block and step with AReA's selection rule~\citep{rajabi2026area}, so each window also sees tokens from the whole image. Keys form a multiset; a bridge token inside the window enters twice. With $w=n_0$ the window is an exact native-size sliding window, which models content spanning a fixed number of tokens: edges, texture, fine structure.

\paragraph{Far view: the global operator.} Let $\Gnat=[H/s_H]\times[W/s_W]$ be the $n_0\times n_0$ grid of blocks. Block-centre subsampling keeps for each block $b$ the token at $\kappa(b)=(s_H b_1+\lfloor s_H/2\rfloor,\, s_W b_2+\lfloor s_W/2\rfloor)$. The coarse map $c(p)=(\lfloor p_1/s_H\rfloor,\lfloor p_2/s_W\rfloor)$ sends a position to its block, so $c(\kappa(b))=b$, and
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_{\kappa(b)},v_{\kappa(b)},b)\}_{b\in\Gnat},\qquad
  \Oglob_i=\Attn\big(q_i,\,c(p_i);\,\Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}\big),
  \label{eq:glob}
\end{equation}
with $c(p_i)=0$ for text queries. This is AReA's first phase at pooling factor $s$, the image as one native canvas. It models content spanning a fixed fraction of the image: layout, object count, identity. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} proves the two propositions below.

\begin{proposition}[Trained position range]
\label{prop:range}
Assume exact integer positions and $t\le n_0\le\min(H,W)$. Every query-key offset of the global operator, and every offset between an image query and a key of its native window $\Win_{n_0}(i)$ (bridge keys and wider windows excluded), is at most $n_0-1$ per axis. Each global-operator query sees the native $T+n_0^2$ keys.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Cost]
\label{prop:cost}
Per head and layer, full attention scores $(N+T)^2$ query-key pairs, the global operator $(N+T)(T+N_0)$ with $N_0=|\Gnat|=n_0^2$, and the local operator $N(T+R+w^2)$ with $R=|\mathcal{B}|$. For fixed $n_0$, $w$, $R$, and $T$ the total is $O\big(N(T+R+w^2+N_0)\big)$, linear in $N$.
\end{proposition}
\paragraph{Worked example.} At $4096^2$ the latent grid is $256\times256$ tokens ($s=4$, $N=65{,}536$, $T=512$). A global-branch query sees 4,096 block centres on a $64\times64$ grid and the text, 4,608 keys; in the native phase each $8\times8$ query tile sees its $64\times64$ window, 1,024 bridge tokens, and the text, \hlWorkedLocalKeys{} keys, against 66,048 at offsets up to 255 in full attention. Over 28 steps \sca{} scores \hlWorkedPairsRatio{} times fewer query-key pairs than full attention (Table~\ref{tab:flops}); Appendix~\ref{app:impl} gives the design choices.

\subsection{Reach emulation}
\label{sec:emulation}
\sca{} mixes the two operators per head, $O_i=\pi\,\Oloc_i+(1-\pi)\,\Oglob_i$ for an image query, and the weight $\pi$ is the head's reach: a near-reaching head takes $\pi$ near 1 and a far-reaching head near 0 (Figure~\ref{fig:focus}a, right). On an in-distribution $n\times n$ grid ($n=64$ at $1024^2$), emulated ratio $r$ uses subsampling factor $r$, window side $n/r$, and tile side $\mathrm{round}(tn/(rn_0))$. The grid-to-window ratio is then $r$, as at a target of scale $r$, with $r^2$ times fewer keys per branch; we assume the weight depends on $r$ alone, which is scale covariance, and test this in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}. The sampler runs unmodified full attention (Algorithm~\ref{alg:emulate}), and at every block and step both emulated operators see the same queries, keys, and values. For block $l$, head $h$, noise bin $\beta$, and ratio $r$, sums over prompts, steps with $\sigma\in\beta$, image queries, and channels give
\begin{equation}
  A=\textstyle\sum\|\Oloc-\Oglob\|^2,\quad
  B=\sum\langle \Ofull-\Oglob,\,\Oloc-\Oglob\rangle,\quad
  E=\sum\|\Ofull-\Oglob\|^2,
  \label{eq:stats}
\end{equation}
where the emulated $\Oloc,\Oglob$ omit the bridge. Weight $\pi$ has squared error $J(\pi)=E-2\pi B+\pi^2A$.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form fit and transfer cost]
\label{prop:opt}
In each cell $(l,h,\beta,r)$, $J$ is minimized on $[0,1]$ by $\pi^\star=\clip(B/A,0,1)$, with $\pi^\star=1$ if $A=0$. Any other weight $\pi'\in[0,1]$ constant in the cell has $J(\pi')-J(\pi^\star)\ge A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2$, with equality when $0<\pi^\star<1$. This covers weights fitted at another ratio or resolution, per layer, or shared, and a hard switch.
\end{proposition}
The same sums score every alternative rule without another forward pass, and one pass collects ratios 2 and 4.

# Input: prompts; full-attention sampler at (*@$1024^2$@*)
# Output: pi*[s_c, l, h, beta]; full in App. (*@\ref{app:code}@*)
def emulate(q, k, v, l, sigma):    # every block
  O_full = attn(q, k, v)        # Eq. (*@\ref{eq:attn}@*), all keys
  beta = min(int(14 * sigma), 13)    # noise bin
  for s_c in (2, 4):            # r = n / w = s_c
    f, w = s_c, n // s_c        # subsample, window
    t_c = round(t * w / n0)     # tile t n/(s_c n0)
    O_glob = global_branch(q, k, v, f)    # Eq. (*@\ref{eq:glob}@*)
    O_loc = local_branch(q, k, v, w, t_c) # Eq. (*@\ref{eq:loc}@*)
    dl, df = O_loc - O_glob, O_full - O_glob
    A[s_c, l, :, beta] += sum(dl * dl)    # Eq. (*@\ref{eq:stats}@*)
    B[s_c, l, :, beta] += sum(df * dl)  # over rows
    E[s_c, l, :, beta] += sum(df * df)
  return O_full                 # sampler unchanged
pi = clip(B / A, 0, 1); pi[A == 0] = 1  # Prop. (*@\ref{prop:opt}@*)
pi = fill_empty_bins(pi)  # read at r, Eq. (*@\ref{eq:sched}@*)

# Output: O for all T + HW queries; App. (*@\ref{app:code}@*)

\subsection{Single-pass sampling: from the global view to the native window}
\label{sec:sched}
The reach table (Section~\ref{sec:emulation}; Algorithm~\ref{alg:emulate}) gives each head $h$ of block $l$ a weight $\pi_{lh}(\sigma;r)$ for every noise level and emulated image-to-window ratio $r\in\{2,4\}$. At the target (Algorithm~\ref{alg:sample}), a window schedule $w(\sigma)$ sets the ratio $r(\sigma)=\max(H,W)/w(\sigma)$, and an image query takes
\begin{equation}
  O_i \;=\; \begin{cases} \Oglob_i & \sigma>\sigma_g,\\ \pi\,\Oloc_i\big(w(\sigma)\big) + (1-\pi)\,\Oglob_i & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad \pi=\pi_{lh}\big(\sigma;r(\sigma)\big),
  \label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_g$ is the gate and text queries take $\Oglob_i$. Between ratios 2 and 4, $\pi$ is linear in $\log r$:
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{lh}(\sigma;r)=(1-\lambda)\,\pi_{lh}(\sigma;2)+\lambda\,\pi_{lh}(\sigma;4),\qquad \lambda=\clip(\log_2 r-1,\,0,\,1).
  \label{eq:sched}
\end{equation}
At inference, each weight is interpolated linearly in $\sigma$ between the mean noise levels of occupied bins (14 of width $1/14$), constant beyond them, and in $\log r$ by Eq.~\ref{eq:sched}. The native phase reads the ratio nearest $\max(s_H,s_W)$ in log space, here always 4. The local operator is one exact FlexAttention call with text and bridge keys as a shared prefix~\citep[Algorithm~\ref{alg:sample}]{dong2024flex}. We use $\sigma_g=0.89$, the first 8 of 28 steps at maximum shift as in AReA. The next four steps use windows of $w=128$, 112, 96, and 80 tokens, one step each, then $w=n_0$ ($\sigma$ bins in Table~\ref{tab:handset}); at $4096^2$ they run at ratios 2, 2.29, 2.67, and 3.2, and the last 16 steps at ratio 4. Setting $w=n_0$ after the gate and dropping the bridge gives a one-step switch, our first operator, which we call \sca{} without the schedule. The four sizes and their $\sigma$ bins were set by hand, and each bin holds one step only for the 28-step sampler; only the weights are fitted (Table~\ref{tab:handset}).

# Input: prompt, seed, table pi; full: Alg. (*@\ref{alg:prog}@*)
sig = schedule(30)            # HRDiT's, all stages

HRDiT's released code~\citep{xue2026hrdit} denoises at $1024^2$ for 30 steps with the model's own attention. It then decodes, upsamples, sharpens, re-encodes, and re-noises the image, and runs the last 17 steps of its schedule again at $2048^2$ and the last 10 at $4096^2$, guided toward the low frequencies of the previous stage. Progressive \sca{} keeps all of this, including guidance and seeds, and replaces HRDiT's four attention changes (NTK-scaled RoPE, a proportional attention scale, per-head key pruning, and position alignment) with \sca{} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:prog_main}). One reach table serves both stages: the $2048^2$ stage reads ratio 2 and the $4096^2$ stage ratio 4. The stages re-enter at $\sigma=0.81$ and $0.61$, below the gate and the last wide window. They run the native window, the bridge, and the fitted per-head mix, and the $1024^2$ stage takes the place of the global opening.

\section{Reach is set by the head}
\label{sec:analysis}

Reach emulation on FLUX.1-dev uses 32 purpose-written prompts at $1024^2$ and 16 at $1280^2$, none from the evaluation sets. Errors are relative, $\sum J/\sum\|\Ofull\|^2$, pooled over blocks, heads, and steps.

\paragraph{Reach varies by head, not by step.} For 256 random queries per block and step we record the share of each head's image attention within 16 tokens of the query. After the first 8 steps, the head accounts for \hlVarHead\% of the variance in this share, and the noise level for \hlVarStep\% (Figure~\ref{fig:focus}c). Most of the head's part lies between heads of the same block. The head-averaged curve accounts for \hlVarAvg\%: it rises from \hlVarCurveLo{} to \hlVarCurveHi{} as denoising proceeds, the narrowing AReA reports. FLUX.1-Krea-dev and FLUX.2-dev give \hlVarHeadKrea\% and \hlVarHeadFluxTwo\% for the head (Table~\ref{tab:variance_split}).

\paragraph{Heads differ, and each head's weight depends on the ratio.} At scale 2, $\pih$ has a standard deviation of 0.24 to 0.26 over the 1368 heads at every noise level, and at $\sigma=0.62$ it tracks each head's full-attention mass within 16 tokens ($\rho=0.77$; Figures~\ref{fig:heads} and~\ref{fig:pi_heads}). The mean weight is lower at ratio 2 than 4 (0.65 against 0.69 at $\sigma=0.90$): wider windows take less local weight.

\paragraph{The table is stable and transfers.} Tables fitted on disjoint prompt halves correlate at 0.989 over all cells (0.986 at scale 4). Applied at scale 4, the scale-2 table gives 0.205 against 0.200 for the scale-4 table, below every shared rule fitted at scale 4; the $1024^2$ table scored at $1280^2$ gives 0.0589 against 0.0585. The two resolutions emulate scale 2 with windows of 32 and 40 tokens, so the weights appear to depend mainly on the image-to-window ratio, as Eq.~\ref{eq:sched} assumes.

\paragraph{Per-head beats coarser rules; output error misses layout.} Against the best hard switch, per-head mixing lowers the relative error from 0.092 to 0.057 at scale 2 and from 0.261 to 0.200 at scale 4. Per-layer tables reach 0.071 and 0.223, and one shared weight 0.077 and 0.230 (Figure~\ref{fig:relerr}). Output error cannot see layout: fitted on it alone, the best hard switch is local attention from the first step, yet the per-head mix without an opening loses 1.33 ImageReward at $4096^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}). The global opening, or the $1024^2$ stage of progressive \sca{}, therefore sets the layout.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} changes one component of single-pass \sca{} at a time (examples in Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_grid}). \textbf{The opening phase is essential.} Without it (v0), ImageReward falls by 1.33 $[1.06,1.60]$, while CLIP-IQA and ARNIQA rise (Table~\ref{tab:app_abl}). \textbf{The per-head mix carries global context after the opening.} Local-only attention in the last 20 steps costs 0.36 $[0.21,0.52]$ ImageReward. \textbf{Per-head weights beat shared and per-layer weights, in the order output error predicts.} One shared weight per noise level lowers ImageReward by 0.09 $[0.01,0.17]$ and MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ by 10.4, and per-layer weights still lose on HPSv2, HPSv2.1, PickScore, ARNIQA, and MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). \textbf{Neither branch works alone.} Global only loses everywhere; mean pooling lowers ImageReward by 1.52. \textbf{The hand-set schedule and bridge change per-image scores little.} Without them, MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ is 1.7 higher and no other column moves significantly. Of three variants of their settings, 2,048 bridge tokens lowers HPSv2.1 and PickScore and a five-step schedule lowers MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ by 0.2.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Training-free high-resolution sampling.} Methods alter convolutions, spectra, or attention temperature~\citep{he2023scalecrafter,zhang2023hidiffusion,huang2024fouriscale,jin2023training}, fuse global and patch paths~\citep{du2023demofusion,yang2024fam}, by frequency in FreeScale~\citep{qiu2024freescale}, add native-resolution guidance~\citep{du2024imax,bu2025hiflow,koh2025scalediff}, or adapt RoPE~(\citealp{zhuo2024lumina,issachar2025dype,zhao2026sigma,rajabi2026sega}; \mbox{\citealp{liu2026tide}}). Multi-stage samplers draw the image at native size and refine it at larger sizes~\citep{du2023demofusion,xue2026hrdit}; progressive \sca{} keeps HRDiT's schedule and changes only its attention. Of the closest methods (Table~\ref{tab:related}, Appendix~\ref{app:related}), AReA~\citep{rajabi2026area} sets one schedule for all heads by hand (in \sca{}'s notation, every head's window weight is 0, then 1); \sca{} borrows its opening, block-centre keys, and bridge selection rule. ResDiT fuses position-scaled global and native-size patch attention between global-only and local-only phases~\citep{ma2025resdit}.

\paragraph{Head- and layer-specific attention.} HRDiT searches per-head scopes under a cost budget for each target size~\citep{xue2026hrdit}; UltraImage types heads by entropy at run time and noted stable local and global head roles before us~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage}. Sparse attention varies windows, spans, or key scales per layer or head in diffusion transformers~\citep{yuan2024ditfastattn,xi2025svg,zhang2025sta}, language models~\citep{fu2024moa,xiao2024duoattention}, and vision transformers~\citep{pan2022hilo}. These settings are often fitted against dense attention at the size where it runs, which at $4096^2$ is the failure; \sca{} fits once, at the training resolution. Head roles, two branches (our local branch is neighbourhood attention, \citealp{hassani2022nat}), a global opening, and shrinking windows have precedents; reach emulation and the measured comparison of heads and steps are ours.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}
\paragraph{Limitations.} \textbf{Fidelity.} Progressive \sca{} trades fine detail for preference against single-pass \sca{} (MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ $\hlProgScaMusiqc$, patch FID $\hlProgScaFidP$). Its patch FID and patch KID are worse than HRDiT's, and its patch FID is higher than AReA's at two of three seeds (Section~\ref{sec:main}). In single-pass mode, small additional figures occasionally appear in scenes with people or animals; the progressive configuration reduces this to the level of the multi-stage baselines. \textbf{Hand-set settings.} The opening, window sizes, and bridge are set by hand because output error cannot see layout (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). We chose the window sizes on the 46-prompt subset, part of the evaluation set. \textbf{Other limits.} Progressive \sca{} depends on HRDiT's schedule, and its sampler does not support FLUX.1-Krea-dev. The $6144^2$, $8192^2$, FLUX.2-dev, and sensitivity results use \sca{} without the schedule. Reach emulation omits the bridge and sees 16 times fewer keys per branch than the target; the deployed operator's error is untested. Metrics are automatic; intervals ignore multiple comparisons.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} A head's reach can be measured once at the training resolution, where full attention is still correct, by shrinking both views to the target's image-to-window ratio. Reach then varies far more between heads than between steps, so \sca{} gives each head its own mix where AReA gives all heads one schedule. Inside HRDiT's schedule, \sca{} is $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA at $4096^2$, beats it on all ten per-image metrics for three seeds and on HRGen-1K, and matches HRDiT's preference scores in less than half its time; in one pass it is $\hlSpeedArea\times$ faster than AReA\ifanynewmodel, and it also runs on \newmodelList.\else{} and keeps more fine detail.\fi{} Code: \codeurl.
