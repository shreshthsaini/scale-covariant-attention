\begin{abstract}
At a diffusion transformer's training resolution, where full attention is still valid, how far a head attends is a property of the head, not of the denoising step: in FLUX.1-dev the head explains \hlVarHead\% of the variance in attention kept near the query, the noise level \hlVarStep\%. Yet training-free high-resolution methods give all heads one schedule or search scopes per target size. We introduce Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}), which mixes two native-size views, a near window and a far grid, with one number per head: its reach. Reach emulation measures it once at $1024^2$ by shrinking both views to the target's image-to-window ratio, so full attention is the reference and the best mix has a closed form. In HRDiT's progressive sampler, \sca{} is $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA at $4096^2$, beats it on all ten per-image metrics at three seeds, and matches HRDiT's preference scores in \hlProgHrditPct\% of its time. Code: \codeurl.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Diffusion transformers~\citep{peebles2022dit} such as FLUX.1~\citep{flux2024} are trained near one megapixel, and every training-free method that samples them at $4096^2$ must decide how far each attention head looks. At that size the image has 16 times as many tokens, full attention costs about 256 times as much, and every layer meets position offsets it never saw. Full attention then returns stripes or a flat field (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). AReA~\citep{rajabi2026area} observes that attention, averaged over heads, narrows as denoising proceeds, and gives every head one schedule: pooled keys for 8 of 28 steps, then windows that shrink to native size. HRDiT~\citep{xue2026hrdit} searches per-head scopes for each target size, and UltraImage~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage} types heads at run time. None of these choices can be checked at the target, where full attention is the failure. We ask whether reach can be measured where full attention is still valid, at the training resolution.

At the training resolution, reach is a property of the head, not of the denoising step (Figure~\ref{fig:observation}). We take a head's reach at a query to be the share of its image attention within 16 tokens. Over 32 prompts at $1024^2$ and the steps after the first 8, the head explains \hlVarHead\% of the variance of this share across FLUX.1-dev's 1,368 heads and noise levels, and the noise level \hlVarStep\%. The head-averaged curve, the most that one schedule for all heads can follow, explains \hlVarAvg\%. UltraImage noted stable local and global head roles~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage}; we give the first decomposition of reach variance into heads and steps.

Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}) turns the observation into one number per head (Figure~\ref{fig:method}). Each head sees two views that keep the training geometry at any size: a native-size window around the query and a native-size grid of block-centre keys over the whole image. Its reach $\pi_h$ mixes them. We measure $\pi_h$ by \emph{reach emulation}. At $1024^2$, shrinking the window and the grid by the target's image-to-window ratio $r$ reproduces the target's proportions, and the model's own full attention there is the reference. The squared error is quadratic in the mix, so $\pi^\star=\clip(B/A,0,1)$ in closed form, and one pass fills a \emph{reach table} for every target size. The table repeats on disjoint prompts (correlation 0.99) and transfers across ratios and resolutions. To our knowledge, no prior method measures per-head reach once at the training resolution and reuses it at any size.

\sca{} replaces only the attention, so any sampler can read the table. In HRDiT's three-stage sampler, progressive \sca{} makes a $4096^2$ FLUX.1-dev image in \hlSecProg\,s on one GH200, $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA and $\hlSpeedProgHrdit\times$ faster than HRDiT (Figure~\ref{fig:speed}). It beats AReA on all ten per-image metrics at each of three seeds and on HRGen-1K, and matches HRDiT's preference scores in \hlProgHrditPct\% of HRDiT's time. A single-pass mode is $\hlSpeedArea\times$ faster than AReA and keeps the most crop-level detail of any method. The progressive schedule costs patch-level fidelity, where HRDiT leads. Per-head weights beat per-layer and shared ones in output error and in images, so the measured reach carries the gain.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Observation.} We show that at the training resolution reach is set by the head, not by the denoising step, in FLUX.1-dev, FLUX.1-Krea-dev, and FLUX.2-dev (Section~\ref{sec:observation}).
  \item \textbf{Method.} We introduce \sca{} and reach emulation, which fits each head's mix of a native window and grid once at $1024^2$, in closed form, for every target size (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{Results.} We show that progressive \sca{} is $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA and better on all ten per-image metrics at three seeds, and that per-head weights beat shared and per-layer ones (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Reach is a property of the head}
\label{sec:observation}\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Reach.} FLUX.1 applies joint attention to text and image tokens in 57 blocks of 24 heads and is trained at about $1024^2$ pixels, a native grid of $64\times64$ image tokens. For one head and a query $i$, splitting the keys into a near set around the query and a far set gives exactly $\Ofull_i=m_iO^{\mathrm{near}}_i+(1-m_i)O^{\mathrm{far}}_i$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:split}). Here $\Ofull_i$ is the full attention output, each $O$ attends to its own set, and $m_i$ is the near set's softmax mass. The near share is the head's reach at that query: a near-reaching head keeps its attention close, a far-reaching head spreads it over the image (Figure~\ref{fig:observation}a). We measure it at $1024^2$, where full attention is valid. FLUX.1-dev samples 32 purpose-written prompts, none from the evaluation sets, and for 256 queries per block and step we record each head's share of image attention within 16 tokens.

\paragraph{The head sets the reach.} After the first 8 steps ($\sigma<0.89$), the head explains \hlVarHead\% of the variance of this share over the 1,368 heads and the noise levels, and the noise level \hlVarStep\% (Figure~\ref{fig:observation}b). Most of the head's share lies between heads of the same block rather than between blocks. The head-averaged curve rises from \hlVarCurveLo{} to \hlVarCurveHi{} as denoising proceeds, the narrowing AReA reports, yet it explains \hlVarAvg\% of the variance, and no schedule shared by all heads can explain more. FLUX.1-Krea-dev and FLUX.2-dev give \hlVarHeadKrea\% and \hlVarHeadFluxTwo\% for the head (Table~\ref{tab:variance_split}). One number per head thus captures nearly all of the variation, and because it barely moves with the step, it can be measured once.

\sca{} gives each head two views that keep the training geometry at any size and mixes them with the head's reach, measured once at $1024^2$ (Figure~\ref{fig:method}).

\subsection{Two native-size views}
\label{sec:setting}\label{sec:branches}

\paragraph{Setting.} A multimodal diffusion transformer such as FLUX.1 concatenates $T$ text tokens $\Tset$ with $N=HW$ image tokens on the grid $\Gset=[H]\times[W]$, where $[n]=\{0,\dots,n-1\}$; its native grid at $1024^2$ pixels is $n_0\times n_0$ tokens with $n_0=64$. Rotary embeddings~\citep{su2021roformer} give token $j$ a position $p_j\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ (FLUX.1 puts all text tokens at $0$) and a rotation $R_p$ with $R_p^\top R_{p'}=R_{p'-p}$. For one head, a pre-rotation query $q$ placed at $u$, and a set $\Kset$ of (key, value, key position) triples,
\begin{equation}
  \Attn(q,u;\Kset) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{(k,v,u')\in\Kset} \operatorname{softmax}_{\Kset}\!\big(q^\top R_{u'-u}\,k/\sqrt d\big)\; v ,
  \label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the head dimension. Full attention $\Ofull_i$ attends to all keys at their positions. At a target grid $H=s_H n_0$, $W=s_W n_0$ ($s$ when equal), full attention meets offsets $s$ times the trained range. The latent at noise level $\sigma$ is $x_\sigma=(1-\sigma)x_0+\sigma\epsilon$.

\paragraph{Near view: the local operator.} The near view attends to a native-size window around the query, the text, and a small bridge set. Image queries form tiles of $t\times t$ tokens ($t=8$); query $i$ lies in tile $\tau=\lfloor p_i/t\rfloor$. The window $\Win_w(i)$ of tile $\tau$ has side $w$, at most the grid side, and starts at $a(\tau)=\mbox{$\clip\big(t\tau+\lfloor t/2\rfloor-\lfloor w/2\rfloor,0,H-w\big)$}$ per axis. Its keys are the text tokens, a bridge set $\mathcal{B}$ of image tokens, and the window, all at their original positions:
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i(w)=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_j,v_j,p_j)\}_{j\in\mathcal{B}\cup\Win_w(i)},\qquad
  \Oloc_i(w)=\Attn\big(q_i,\,p_i;\,\Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i(w)\big).
  \label{eq:loc}
\end{equation}
The bridge takes, in each cell $c$ of an $8\times8$ partition of the grid, the 16 tokens whose pre-rotation keys lie farthest from the mean image key $\bar k^{h}$, summed over heads $h$, with AReA's selection rule~\citep{rajabi2026area}, so each window also sees the whole image:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{B}=\textstyle\bigcup_{c}\operatorname{top}_{16}\big\{\sum_{h}\|k^{h}_j-\bar k^{h}\|^2 : j\in c\big\}.
  \label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
With $w=n_0$ the window is an exact native-size sliding window, for content that spans a fixed number of tokens: edges, texture, fine structure.

\paragraph{Far view: the global operator.} The far view attends to one key per block of a native-size grid over the whole image. Let $\Gnat=[H/s_H]\times[W/s_W]$ be the $n_0\times n_0$ grid of blocks. Block-centre subsampling keeps for each block $b$ the token at $\kappa(b)=(s_H b_1+\lfloor s_H/2\rfloor,\, s_W b_2+\lfloor s_W/2\rfloor)$, and the coarse map $c(p)=(\lfloor p_1/s_H\rfloor,\lfloor p_2/s_W\rfloor)$ sends a position to its block:
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_{\kappa(b)},v_{\kappa(b)},b)\}_{b\in\Gnat},\qquad
  \Oglob_i=\Attn\big(q_i,\,c(p_i);\,\Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}\big),
  \label{eq:glob}
\end{equation}
with $c(p_i)=0$ for text queries. This is AReA's first phase at pooling factor $s$, the image as one native canvas, for content that spans a fixed fraction of the image: layout, object count, identity. Every offset of the grid and of the native window stays in the trained range, and the cost is linear in $N$ (Propositions~\ref{prop:range} and~\ref{prop:cost}, Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Over 28 steps at $4096^2$, \sca{} scores \hlWorkedPairsRatio{} times fewer query-key pairs than full attention (Table~\ref{tab:flops}).

\paragraph{One number per head.} An image query of head $h$ takes $O_i=\pi_h\,\Oloc_i+(1-\pi_h)\,\Oglob_i$. The weight $\pi_h$ is the head's reach: near 1 for a near-reaching head, near 0 for a far-reaching one.

\subsection{Reach emulation}
\label{sec:emulation}

\paragraph{Full attention at $1024^2$ is the reference.} At the target, full attention fails, so nothing there can score $\pi_h$; reach emulation scores it at $1024^2$ (Figure~\ref{fig:method}b). On the native $n\times n$ grid ($n=64$), emulated ratio $r$ uses subsampling factor $r$, window side $n/r$, and tile side $\mathrm{round}(tn/(rn_0))$. The image-to-window ratio is then $r$, as at a target $r$ times larger, with $r^2$ times fewer keys per view. The sampler runs unmodified full attention (Algorithm~\ref{alg:emulate}), and at every block and step both emulated views see the same queries, keys, and values. For block $l$, head $h$, noise bin $\beta$, and ratio $r$, sums over prompts, steps with $\sigma\in\beta$, image queries, and channels give
\begin{equation}
  A=\textstyle\sum\|\Oloc-\Oglob\|^2,\quad
  B=\sum\langle \Ofull-\Oglob,\,\Oloc-\Oglob\rangle,\quad
  E=\sum\|\Ofull-\Oglob\|^2,
  \label{eq:stats}
\end{equation}
where the emulated $\Oloc,\Oglob$ omit the bridge. Weight $\pi$ has squared error $J(\pi)=E-2\pi B+\pi^2A$.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form fit and transfer cost]
\label{prop:opt}
In each cell $(l,h,\beta,r)$, $J$ is minimized on $[0,1]$ by $\pi^\star=\clip(B/A,0,1)$, with $\pi^\star=1$ if $A=0$. Any other weight $\pi'\in[0,1]$ constant in the cell has $J(\pi')-J(\pi^\star)\ge A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2$, with equality when $0<\pi^\star<1$. This covers weights fitted at another ratio or resolution, per layer, or shared, and a hard switch.
\end{proposition}
One pass collects ratios 2 and 4 and fills the reach table $\pi_{lh}(\sigma;r)$; the same sums score every alternative rule without another forward pass. We assume the weight depends on $r$ and not on the absolute size, which is scale covariance.

\paragraph{One table serves every size.} Tables fitted on disjoint prompt halves correlate at 0.989 over all cells (0.986 at ratio 4). The fitted weight tracks each head's full-attention mass within 16 tokens ($\rho=0.77$ at $\sigma=0.62$; Figure~\ref{fig:heads}). Applied at ratio 4, the ratio-2 table gives relative error 0.205 against 0.200 for the ratio-4 table, below every shared rule fitted at ratio 4. The $1024^2$ table scored at $1280^2$ gives 0.0589 against 0.0585. The two resolutions emulate ratio 2 with windows of 32 and 40 tokens, so the weights appear to depend mainly on the image-to-window ratio, as scale covariance assumes. Relative errors are $\sum J/\sum\|\Ofull\|^2$, pooled over blocks, heads, and steps, on 32 prompts at $1024^2$ and 16 at $1280^2$.

# Input: prompts; full-attention sampler at (*@$1024^2$@*)
# Output: pi*[s_c, l, h, beta]; full in App. (*@\ref{app:code}@*)
def emulate(q, k, v, l, sigma):    # every block
  O_full = attn(q, k, v)        # Eq. (*@\ref{eq:attn}@*), all keys
  beta = min(int(14 * sigma), 13)    # noise bin
  for s_c in (2, 4):            # r = n / w = s_c
    f, w = s_c, n // s_c        # subsample, window
    t_c = round(t * w / n0)     # tile t n/(s_c n0)
    O_glob = global_branch(q, k, v, f)    # Eq. (*@\ref{eq:glob}@*)
    O_loc = local_branch(q, k, v, w, t_c) # Eq. (*@\ref{eq:loc}@*)
    dl, df = O_loc - O_glob, O_full - O_glob
    A[s_c, l, :, beta] += sum(dl * dl)    # Eq. (*@\ref{eq:stats}@*)
    B[s_c, l, :, beta] += sum(df * dl)  # over rows
    E[s_c, l, :, beta] += sum(df * df)
  return O_full                 # sampler unchanged
pi = clip(B / A, 0, 1); pi[A == 0] = 1  # Prop. (*@\ref{prop:opt}@*)
pi = fill_empty_bins(pi)  # read at r, Eq. (*@\ref{eq:sched}@*)

\subsection{Sampling with the reach table}
\label{sec:sched}\label{sec:progressive}

\paragraph{Progressive \sca{}.} Our main configuration keeps HRDiT's released three-stage sampler~\citep{xue2026hrdit} and replaces only its four attention changes (NTK-scaled RoPE, a proportional attention scale, per-head key pruning, and position alignment) with \sca{} (Figure~\ref{fig:method}c). HRDiT denoises at $1024^2$ for 30 steps with the model's own attention. It then decodes, upsamples, sharpens, re-encodes, and re-noises the image. It runs the last 17 steps of its schedule at $2048^2$ and the last 10 at $4096^2$, guided toward the low frequencies of the previous stage (Algorithm~\ref{alg:prog}, Appendix~\ref{app:code}). The $2048^2$ stage reads the table at ratio 2 and the $4096^2$ stage at ratio 4. Both re-enter at $\sigma=0.81$ and $0.61$ with the native window, the bridge, and the per-head mix, and the $1024^2$ stage sets the layout.

\paragraph{Single-pass mode.} \sca{} also samples at the target in one pass of 28 steps. A window schedule $w(\sigma)$ sets the ratio $r(\sigma)=\max(H,W)/w(\sigma)$, and an image query takes
\begin{equation}
  O_i \;=\; \begin{cases} \Oglob_i & \sigma>\sigma_g,\\ \pi\,\Oloc_i\big(w(\sigma)\big) + (1-\pi)\,\Oglob_i & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad \pi=\pi_{lh}\big(\sigma;r(\sigma)\big),
  \label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
where text queries take $\Oglob_i$. Between ratios 2 and 4, $\pi$ is linear in $\log r$, and in $\sigma$ it is interpolated between occupied noise bins:
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{lh}(\sigma;r)=(1-\lambda)\,\pi_{lh}(\sigma;2)+\lambda\,\pi_{lh}(\sigma;4),\qquad \lambda=\clip(\log_2 r-1,\,0,\,1).
  \label{eq:sched}
\end{equation}
The global view runs alone while $\sigma>\sigma_g=0.89$, the first 8 of 28 steps as in AReA, because output error cannot see layout (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}). Windows of 128, 112, 96, and 80 tokens then take one step each before the native $w=n_0$. The gate, the window sizes, and the 1,024 bridge tokens are set by hand; only the weights are measured (Table~\ref{tab:handset}). With $w=n_0$ right after the gate and no bridge, the operator is \sca{} without the schedule. The local view is one FlexAttention call with text and bridge keys as a shared prefix~\citep{dong2024flex}.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Training-free high-resolution sampling.} Methods alter convolutions, spectra, or attention temperature~\citep{he2023scalecrafter,zhang2023hidiffusion,huang2024fouriscale,jin2023training}, fuse global and patch paths~\citep{du2023demofusion,yang2024fam}, by frequency in FreeScale~\citep{qiu2024freescale}, add native-resolution guidance~\citep{du2024imax,bu2025hiflow,koh2025scalediff}, or adapt RoPE~(\citealp{zhuo2024lumina,issachar2025dype,zhao2026sigma,rajabi2026sega}; \mbox{\citealp{liu2026tide}}). Multi-stage samplers draw the image at native size and refine it at larger sizes~\citep{du2023demofusion,xue2026hrdit}; progressive \sca{} keeps HRDiT's sampler and changes only its attention. AReA~\citep{rajabi2026area} sets one schedule for all heads by hand, in \sca{}'s notation a window weight of 0 and then 1 for every head. \sca{} borrows its opening, block-centre keys, and bridge selection rule (Table~\ref{tab:related}, Appendix~\ref{app:related}). ResDiT fuses position-scaled global and native-size patch attention between global-only and local-only phases~\citep{ma2025resdit}.

\paragraph{Head-specific attention.} HRDiT searches per-head scopes under a cost budget for each target size~\citep{xue2026hrdit}, and UltraImage types heads by entropy at run time and noted stable local and global head roles~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage}. Sparse attention varies windows, spans, or key scales per layer or head in diffusion transformers~\citep{yuan2024ditfastattn,xi2025svg,zhang2025sta}, language models~\citep{fu2024moa,xiao2024duoattention}, and vision transformers~\citep{pan2022hilo}. These settings are usually fitted against dense attention at the size where it runs, which at $4096^2$ is the failure. Head roles, two branches (our local branch is neighbourhood attention,~\citealp{hassani2022nat}), a global opening, and shrinking windows have precedents. The decomposition of reach variance into heads and steps, and reach emulation, which measures each head's reach once at the training resolution for every size, are ours.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}
\paragraph{Limitations.} Progressive \sca{} trades patch-level fidelity for preference (Section~\ref{sec:main}). In single-pass mode, small additional figures occasionally appear in scenes with people or animals; the progressive configuration reduces this to the level of the multi-stage baselines. The opening, window sizes, and bridge are set by hand because output error cannot see layout, and we chose the window sizes on the 46-prompt subset, part of the evaluation set. Progressive \sca{} depends on HRDiT's sampler, which does not support FLUX.1-Krea-dev, and the $6144^2$, $8192^2$, FLUX.2-dev, and sensitivity results use \sca{} without the schedule. Reach emulation omits the bridge and sees 16 times fewer keys per view than the target; the deployed operator's error is untested. Metrics are automatic, and intervals ignore multiple comparisons.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} At the training resolution a head's reach is set by the head and barely moves with the denoising step. It can therefore be measured once, where full attention is still valid, and reused at any size. \sca{} turns it into one number per head, fitted in closed form by reach emulation. Inside HRDiT's sampler it is $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA at $4096^2$, beats it on all ten per-image metrics at three seeds and on HRGen-1K, and matches HRDiT's preference scores in \hlProgHrditPct\% of its time. In one pass it is $\hlSpeedArea\times$ faster than AReA\ifanynewmodel, and it also runs on \newmodelList.\else{} with more fine detail.\fi{} Code: \codeurl.
