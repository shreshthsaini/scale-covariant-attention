\begin{abstract}
Diffusion transformers sampled far above their training resolution see more tokens and longer relative positions than in training; at $4096^2$, full attention in FLUX.1-dev costs about 256 times more than at $1024^2$, and its images collapse into stripes. Training-free fixes restrict which keys a query sees, but give every head the same receptive field on a hand-set schedule. We measure what each head needs instead. At the training resolution, where full attention can be trusted, we emulate a larger image by shrinking a native-extent window and a subsampled view of the whole image by the same factor, and fit in closed form how much of each head's output each view explains. In FLUX.1-dev this split differs strongly between heads, is reproduced by disjoint prompt sets (split-half correlation 0.99), and transfers across emulated scales and calibration resolutions at no more than 5.4\% extra output error. Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}) applies the fitted table at the target resolution: each head mixes a global branch on the native grid with an exact sliding window, and both branches keep the native key count and image offsets. An opening phase uses the global branch alone, because no window can resolve layout at high noise. \sca{} needs one calibration pass of about 1.5 GPU-hours and no training, and its attention cost is linear in the number of tokens. On 191 Aesthetic-4K prompts at $4096^2$, under one protocol shared with AReA's released code, \sca{} raises ImageReward from 0.92 to 1.23 and HPSv2.1 from 27.2 to 29.7, lowers FID from 146.0 to 138.4, is significantly better on every preference, alignment, and no-reference quality metric we report, and runs 3.0 times faster than AReA and 2.6 times faster than full attention. The gain grows with the scale factor: \sca{} ties AReA at $2048\times4096$ and raises ImageReward from 0.39 to 0.66 at $2048\times6144$. \todo{E1, E8: one sentence on RoPE baselines and the second model.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Text-to-image diffusion transformers such as FLUX.1~\citep{flux2024} and Stable Diffusion~3~\citep{esser2024sd3} are trained at about one megapixel. At $4096\times4096$ the number of image tokens grows 16-fold and the cost of full attention about 256-fold, and every attention layer sees a token count and relative positions outside its training distribution. Outputs show duplicated subjects, broken layouts, and blurred texture~\citep{he2023scalecrafter,du2023demofusion,issachar2025dype,rajabi2026area}; under our protocol, full attention in FLUX.1-dev at $4096^2$ gives fields of stripes (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}, bottom row).

Training-free fixes edit the positional encoding~\citep{zhuo2024lumina,issachar2025dype,zhao2025ultraimage,rajabi2026sega} or restrict the keys each query sees~\citep{zhang2023hidiffusion,ma2025resdit,xue2026hrdit,rajabi2026area}. AReA~\citep{rajabi2026area}, the most recent of the second kind, attends to keys subsampled onto a native-size grid for the first 8 of 28 steps, then to windows that shrink to native size, with several repairs for windowing artifacts. Every head of every layer gets the same receptive field at a given step, on a schedule set by hand from attention statistics averaged over heads and layers.

We ask the model instead. At its training resolution, how much of each head's output does a native-size neighbourhood explain, and how much does a downsampled view of the whole image explain? We emulate a target $s$ times larger by shrinking both views by $s$, so that the ratio of image to window matches the target while full attention stays in distribution and serves as ground truth. A convex mix of the two views fits each head well. Its weight differs strongly between heads, is a property of the model rather than of the prompts (split-half correlation 0.99), and transfers: the table fitted at emulated scale 2 explains scale 4 with 2.4\% more output error than the scale-4 table, and a $1024^2$ table explains $1280^2$ with 0.8\% more (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). We call this scale covariance: as the image grows, the local view follows a law fixed in tokens, the global view a law fixed in image fractions, and the weight between them stays put.

Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}) turns this measurement into an attention operator (Figure~\ref{fig:method}). Each head mixes a global branch, which attends to block-centre keys on the native grid with rescaled coordinates, and a local branch, an exact native-extent sliding window, with its calibrated weight. Both branches keep the native key count and every image query-key offset inside the trained range (Proposition~\ref{prop:range}), their cost is linear in the number of tokens (Proposition~\ref{prop:cost}), and the calibrated weight is the $\ell_2$-optimal mix at the emulated scale (Proposition~\ref{prop:opt}). Output matching cannot set the opening steps: applied from the first step at $4096^2$, the calibrated mix draws five Moai statues for a prompt asking for one (Figure~\ref{fig:failure}), because at high noise only the lowest spatial frequencies carry signal and a 64-token window spans a quarter of the image (Argument~\ref{arg:freq}). \sca{} therefore runs the global branch alone for the first 8 of 28 steps. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A calibration principle} for training-free resolution extrapolation: emulate the target scale inside the training distribution and fit each head's receptive-field mix to the model's own full attention in closed form, from statistics collected in one pass. On FLUX.1-dev the mix is head-specific, prompt-stable, and transfers across scale and calibration resolution.
  \item \textbf{The \sca{} operator}: a per-head, per-noise-level mix of two branches that stay in the trained position range, an opening phase motivated by a frequency argument, and an exact FlexAttention sliding window; one attention layer at $4096^2$ takes 18.7\,ms against 87.3\,ms for full attention.
  \item \textbf{An evaluation under one shared protocol} with paired bootstrap intervals for every metric. On Aesthetic-4K, \sca{} improves every preference and quality metric over AReA's released code at $4096^2$ in a third of its time, and its advantage grows with the scale factor; \todo{E1: DyPE, SEGA, UltraImage}; \todo{E2: $6144^2$, $8192^2$}; \todo{E4: HRGen-1K}; \todo{E8, E9: FLUX.2-dev, FLUX.1-Krea-dev}.
  \item \textbf{Ablations} showing that the opening phase, the per-head weights, and block-centre subsampling are each needed, and that per-layer and shared weights lose in the order their output error predicts; \todo{E5, E6, E7: seeds, calibration budget, gate sensitivity}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scale-Covariant Attention}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setting and notation}
\label{sec:setting}
A multimodal diffusion transformer such as FLUX.1 concatenates $T$ text tokens $\Tset$ with $N=HW$ image tokens on the grid $\Gset=[H]\times[W]$, $[n]=\{0,\dots,n-1\}$, and applies joint attention in each block (57 blocks of 24 heads in FLUX.1). It is trained at about $1024^2$ pixels, a native grid of $n_0\times n_0$ tokens with $n_0=64$. Rotary embeddings give token $j$ a position $p_j\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ (FLUX.1 puts all text tokens at $0$) and a rotation $R_p$ with $R_p^\top R_{p'}=R_{p'-p}$. For one head, a pre-rotation query $q$ placed at $u$, and a set $\Kset$ of (key, value, key position) triples,
\begin{equation}
  \Attn(q,u;\Kset) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{(k,v,u')\in\Kset} \operatorname{softmax}_{\Kset}\!\big(q^\top R_{u'-u}\,k/\sqrt d\big)\; v .
  \label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
Full attention is $\Ofull_i=\Attn\big(q_i,p_i;\{(k_j,v_j,p_j)\}_{j\in\Tset\cup\Gset}\big)$. The target grid is $H=s_H n_0$, $W=s_W n_0$ with integer factors ($s$ when equal), so full attention meets offsets $s$ times the trained range. The latent at noise level $\sigma$ is $x_\sigma=(1-\sigma)x_0+\sigma\epsilon$. Softmax attention over a partition of the keys into $A$ and $B$ splits exactly as $\Ofull_i=m_iO^A_i+(1-m_i)O^B_i$, with $m_i$ the softmax mass on $A$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:split}): every head is a query-dependent mix of a near and a far field. \sca{} replaces both restricted outputs with in-distribution operators and $m_i$ with a calibrated per-head weight.

\subsection{Two in-distribution operators}
\label{sec:branches}

\paragraph{Global operator.} Let $\Gnat=[H/s_H]\times[W/s_W]$ be the $n_0\times n_0$ grid of blocks. Block-centre subsampling keeps for each block $b$ the token at $\kappa(b)=(s_H b_1+\lfloor s_H/2\rfloor,\, s_W b_2+\lfloor s_W/2\rfloor)$, and the coarse map $c(p)=(\lfloor p_1/s_H\rfloor,\lfloor p_2/s_W\rfloor)$ sends a position to its block, so $c(\kappa(b))=b$:
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_{\kappa(b)},v_{\kappa(b)},b)\}_{b\in\Gnat},\qquad
  \Oglob_i=\Attn\big(q_i,\,c(p_i);\,\Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}\big),
  \label{eq:glob}
\end{equation}
with $c(p_i)$ replaced by 0 for text queries. This is AReA's first phase with the pooling factor fixed at $s$. It models content whose extent is a fixed fraction of the image: layout, object count, identity.

\paragraph{Local operator.} Image queries form tiles of $t\times t$ tokens ($t=8$); query $i$ lies in tile $\tau=\lfloor p_i/t\rfloor$. With window side $w=\min(n_0,H)$, the window of tile $\tau$ starts at $a(\tau)=\clip\big(t\tau+\lfloor t/2\rfloor-\lfloor w/2\rfloor,0,H-w\big)$ per axis, centred on the tile and clamped to the grid. With $\Win(i)$ the $w\times w$ window of $i$'s tile,
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_j,v_j,p_j)\}_{p_j\in\Win(i)},\qquad
  \Oloc_i=\Attn\big(q_i,\,p_i;\,\Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i\big),
  \label{eq:loc}
\end{equation}
an exact sliding window with the original positions. It models content whose extent is fixed in tokens: edges, texture, fine structure.

\begin{proposition}[Trained position range]
\label{prop:range}
Let $t\le n_0\le\min(H,W)$. Every query-key offset of the global operator and every image query-key offset of the local operator is at most $n_0-1$ per axis, and each image query sees $T+n_0^2$ keys in either operator, as in full attention on the native grid.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Cost]
\label{prop:cost}
Per head and layer, full attention scores $(N+T)^2$ query-key pairs, the global operator $(N+T)(T+N_0)$ with $N_0=|\Gnat|=n_0^2$, and the local operator $N(T+w^2)$. For fixed $n_0$ and $T$ the total is $O\big(N(T+w^2+N_0)\big)$, linear in $N$.
\end{proposition}
Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. Text-image offsets in the local operator equal $p_i$, as in full attention at the target (Remark~\ref{rem:text}). At $4096^2$ \sca{} scores 7.2 times fewer pairs than full attention, 8.4 times fewer with the opening phase (Table~\ref{tab:flops}).

\subsection{Per-head mixing and the opening phase}
\label{sec:mix}
For an image query of head $h$ in block $l$ at noise level $\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
  O_i \;=\; \Oglob_i \;\;\text{if } \sigma>\sigma_g,\qquad
  O_i \;=\; \pi_{lh}(\sigma)\,\Oloc_i + \big(1-\pi_{lh}(\sigma)\big)\,\Oglob_i \;\;\text{otherwise},
  \label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
and text queries use $\Oglob_i$. The weight $\pi_{lh}(\sigma)$, written $\pih$, stands in for the near-field mass $m_i$; one weight per head is cheap and a few prompts determine it.

\begin{argument}[Why output matching cannot set the opening steps]
\label{arg:freq}
At noise level $\sigma$ a spatial frequency band has signal-to-noise ratio $\big((1-\sigma)/\sigma\big)^2 S(f)$ for image spectrum $S$. Natural spectra fall with frequency, so early on only the lowest band carries signal, and it fixes layout and object count. A window of $w$ tokens holds less than one period of any frequency below $1/w$, so it cannot tell one large object from a repeating pattern; neighbouring windows settle the question independently and duplicate subjects at the window's scale. The global operator sees the whole image and resolves this band. Squared output error is dominated by texture, so calibration cannot detect the problem.
\end{argument}
We have not measured the per-band signal-to-noise ratio of FLUX.1 latents, but the argument's prediction holds: a 16-token window gives smaller and more numerous copies than a 64-token one (Figure~\ref{fig:failure}). We set $\sigma_g=0.89$, the first 8 of 28 steps at maximum shift, which is also AReA's switch point; gating 13 steps gives a $4\times4$ block mosaic, because all queries of a block share one coarse position.

\subsection{Calibration by output matching}
\label{sec:calib}
The weights must come from where the model is trustworthy, which the target is not. On an in-distribution $n\times n$ grid ($n=64$ at $1024^2$), emulated scale $s_c$ uses subsampling factor $s_c$, window side $n/s_c$, and tile side $\mathrm{round}(tn/(s_cn_0))$, so that the ratio of grid to window is $s_c$, as at a target of scale $s_c$. The sampler runs unmodified full attention; at every block and step both emulated operators are evaluated on the same queries, keys, and values. For block $l$, head $h$, and noise bin $\beta$, sums over prompts, steps with $\sigma\in\beta$, image queries, and channels give
\begin{equation}
  A=\textstyle\sum\|\Oloc-\Oglob\|^2,\quad
  B=\sum\langle \Ofull-\Oglob,\,\Oloc-\Oglob\rangle,\quad
  E=\sum\|\Ofull-\Oglob\|^2,
  \label{eq:stats}
\end{equation}
and the squared error of the mix with weight $\pi$ is $J(\pi)=E-2\pi B+\pi^2A$.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form calibration and transfer cost]
\label{prop:opt}
In each cell $(l,h,\beta)$, $\pi^\star=\clip(B/A,0,1)$ ($\pi^\star=1$ if $A=0$) minimizes $J$ on $[0,1]$. Any other weight $\pi'\in[0,1]$ (fitted at another scale or resolution, per layer, shared, or a hard switch) has $J(\pi')-J(\pi^\star)\ge A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2$, with equality when $0<\pi^\star<1$.
\end{proposition}
So the per-head table has the lowest output error of any rule constant within a cell, and replacing it costs an $A$-weighted squared distance. This holds at the emulation scale; Section~\ref{sec:analysis} measures empirically how well tables transfer between scales and resolutions. The same sums score every alternative rule without another forward pass. We use 14 bins of width $1/14$ in $\sigma$ and interpolate linearly between bin centres at inference. One pass collects emulated scales 2 and 4; calibration took about 1.5 GPU-hours on one GH200 for 32 prompts at $1024^2$ and 16 at $1280^2$. A target with factors $(s_H,s_W)$ uses the table whose scale is nearest $\max(s_H,s_W)$ in log space, here always 4. The local operator is one FlexAttention call~\citep{dong2024flex} over tile-major tokens with the window membership as block mask, so the window is exact. Algorithm~\ref{alg:sca} and implementation details are in Appendix~\ref{app:impl}.

\section{What the heads want}
\label{sec:analysis}

We calibrate FLUX.1-dev on 32 prompts at $1024^2$ and 16 at $1280^2$, written for this purpose and disjoint from the evaluation sets. Errors are relative, $\sum J/\sum\|\Ofull\|^2$, pooled over blocks, heads, and steps.

\snippet{calib}{%
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/pi_heatmap.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Within every block, heads range from almost purely global to almost purely local.} Fitted $\pih$ for all 57 blocks and 24 heads of FLUX.1-dev at three noise levels, emulated scale 2, 32 prompts. Heads are sorted within each block. The white line separates double-stream from single-stream blocks.}
  \label{fig:calib}
\end{figure}}

\paragraph{Heads differ.} Over the 1368 heads, the standard deviation of $\pih$ is 0.24 to 0.26 at every noise level (emulated scale 2); half of all (head, noise level) cells lie in $[0.2,0.8]$ (50.4\%; 49.7\% at scale 4), 11.7\% exceed 0.95, and 2.9\% are below 0.05 (Figure~\ref{fig:calib}). The mean weight is 0.64 to 0.74 (0.68 to 0.77 at scale 4): the typical head leans local but still draws on the global branch.

\paragraph{The table is a property of the model.} Tables fitted on disjoint halves of the prompts correlate at 0.989 over all cells (0.986 at scale 4; 0.974 for 16 prompts at $1280^2$), and each half's table scored on the other half gives a relative error of 0.0576 against 0.0574 in sample. \todo{E6: tables from 4, 8, 16 prompts.}

\paragraph{The weights barely depend on scale or calibration resolution.} The scale-2 table applied at scale 4 gives a relative error of 0.205 against 0.200 for the scale-4 table, still below every shared rule fitted at scale 4. The $1024^2$ table scored at $1280^2$ gives 0.0589 against 0.0585. Over all pairs of calibration targets, a transferred table adds at most 5.4\% error, while each target's own per-layer fit adds 12 to 24\%. Since the two resolutions emulate scale 2 with windows of 32 and 40 tokens, the weights follow the ratio of image to window, not the window's size.

\paragraph{Per-head beats coarser rules; output error misses layout.} Relative to the best hard switch, per-head mixing lowers the relative error from 0.092 to 0.057 at scale 2 and from 0.261 to 0.200 at scale 4; per-layer tables reach 0.071 and 0.223, one shared weight per noise level 0.077 and 0.230 (Figure~\ref{fig:relerr}). In output energy the best hard switch never switches (Appendix~\ref{app:calib}); the opening phase overrides this on purpose (Argument~\ref{arg:freq}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Training-free high resolution.} Earlier methods change convolutions, spectra, or attention temperature~\citep{he2023scalecrafter,zhang2023hidiffusion,huang2024fouriscale,jin2023training}, fuse global and patch paths~\citep{du2023demofusion,yang2024fam,qiu2024freescale}, or guide with a native pass~\citep{du2024imax,bu2025hiflow,koh2025scalediff}. RoPE methods for diffusion transformers~\citep{zhuo2024lumina,issachar2025dype,zhao2025ultraimage,zhao2026sigma,rajabi2026sega,liu2026tide} rescale positions but keep full attention and its quadratic cost; \sca{} keeps the trained positions and changes the key set. ResDiT fuses a global branch with patch attention by fixed weights~\citep{ma2025resdit}, HRDiT gives each head a static local scope from an integer program under a cost budget~\citep{xue2026hrdit}, AReA schedules one receptive field for all heads~\citep{rajabi2026area}, and FreeScale fuses attention outputs by frequency band~\citep{qiu2024freescale}; \sca{}'s weights are per head and per noise level and come from the model's own full attention. \textbf{Efficient attention.} ToDo attends to downsampled keys without rescaling coordinates~\citep{smith2024todo}; DiTFastAttn, Sparse VideoGen, Sliding Tile Attention, and Sparse-vDiT pick windows or sparsity per layer or head at trained resolutions~\citep{yuan2024ditfastattn,xi2025svg,zhang2025sta,chen2025sparsevdit}; our local branch is neighbourhood attention~\citep{hassani2022nat}. \textbf{Per-head scope elsewhere.} Self-Extend groups distant positions~\citep{jin2024selfextend}, MoA assigns per-head spans~\citep{fu2024moa}, and DuoAttention separates retrieval heads from local ones~\citep{xiao2024duoattention}; HiLo~\citep{pan2022hilo} and Shunted attention~\citep{ren2021shunted} train vision transformers whose heads split fine and coarse keys by design. A full-attention diffusion transformer appears to learn such a split implicitly.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}
\sca{} only ties AReA at $2048\times4096$. Small extra figures can still appear late in sampling, and one development prompt keeps doubled limbs in every variant (Appendix~\ref{app:failures}). The opening phase is set from generations, since output error cannot set it. Transfer across emulated scales supports, but cannot prove, that the weights depend only on the ratio of image to window. Main results use one model and one seed per prompt \todo{E5, E8, E9}.

Each head's split between a neighbourhood fixed in tokens and a view of the whole image can be measured once at the training resolution and reused at 4K; with it and an opening phase, \sca{} beats AReA's released code at $4096^2$ in a third of its time.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{lemma}[Softmax split]
\label{lem:split}
Let the keys of a query be partitioned into $A$ and $B$, with logits $\ell_j$. Then $\Ofull=m\,O^A+(1-m)\,O^B$ with $m=\sum_{j\in A}e^{\ell_j}/\sum_{j}e^{\ell_j}$ and $O^A,O^B$ the softmax outputs restricted to $A$ and $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $Z_A=\sum_{j\in A}e^{\ell_j}$ and $Z_B$ likewise. Then $\Ofull=(\sum_{A}e^{\ell_j}v_j+\sum_{B}e^{\ell_j}v_j)/(Z_A+Z_B)=\frac{Z_A}{Z_A+Z_B}O^A+\frac{Z_B}{Z_A+Z_B}O^B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:range}]
\emph{Global operator.} Image queries sit at $c(p_i)\in\Gnat=[n_0]^2$ and text queries at $0\in\Gnat$; subsampled keys sit at $b\in\Gnat$ and text keys at $0$. Any two points of $[n_0]^2$ differ by at most $n_0-1$ per axis. The key count is $T+|\Gnat|=T+n_0^2$.

\emph{Local operator.} Fix one axis of length $H\ge w$ and a tile $[t\tau,\,t\tau+t)$ with $t\le w$. The unclamped start $a'=t\tau+\lfloor t/2\rfloor-\lfloor w/2\rfloor$ satisfies $a'\le t\tau$ because $\lfloor t/2\rfloor\le\lfloor w/2\rfloor$, and $a'+w\ge t\tau+t$ because $w-\lfloor w/2\rfloor=\lceil w/2\rceil\ge\lceil t/2\rceil=t-\lfloor t/2\rfloor$. If $a'<0$ the window is $[0,w)$, and $w\ge a'+w\ge t\tau+t$. If $a'>H-w$ the window is $[H-w,H)$, with $H-w<a'\le t\tau$ and $H\ge t\tau+t$. In every case the window contains the tile, so it contains each query of the tile, and two positions in a window of side $w\le n_0$ differ by at most $n_0-1$. The key count is $T+w^2=T+n_0^2$ when $n_0\le\min(H,W)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Text-image offsets]
\label{rem:text}
FLUX.1 places all text tokens at position 0, so the offset between an image query at $p_i$ and a text key is $p_i$. In the global operator the query sits at $c(p_i)\in[n_0]^2$, inside the trained range. In the local operator it keeps $p_i$, up to $sn_0-1$ per axis, exactly as in full attention and AReA's windows at the target. Mapping text offsets through $c(\cdot)$ in the local operator is a possible refinement that we have not tested.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cost}]
Full attention scores every query against every key, $(N+T)^2$ pairs. The global operator scores $N+T$ queries against $T+|\Gnat|$ keys with $|\Gnat|=N/(s_Hs_W)=n_0^2$. The local operator scores $N$ image queries against $T$ text keys and $w^2$ window keys. The sum is at most $(2N+T)(T+w^2+N_0)$, which is $O(N(T+w^2+N_0))$ for fixed $n_0,T$. Value aggregation has the same pair counts. In the opening phase the local term is absent. The FlexAttention kernel visits whole blocks and masks the extra pairs: a query block of $8\times16$ tokens spans two tiles whose windows are 8 columns apart, so it visits a $64\times72$ key region, 12.5\% more than one window, plus partial blocks at clamped borders. This changes the constant, not the order.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:opt}]
$J(\pi)=E-2\pi B+\pi^2A$ with $A\ge0$. If $A=0$ then $\Oloc=\Oglob$ on every summand, so $B=0$ and $J$ is constant; any $\pi$, in particular $\pi^\star=1$, is optimal and the bound holds trivially. If $A>0$, $J$ is a strictly convex quadratic with unconstrained minimizer $B/A$, and its minimizer on $[0,1]$ is the projection $\clip(B/A,0,1)$. For any $\pi'$,
\[
J(\pi')-J(\pi^\star)=A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2+2(\pi'-\pi^\star)(A\pi^\star-B).
\]
If $0<\pi^\star<1$ then $A\pi^\star=B$ and the second term vanishes. If $\pi^\star=0$ then $B\le0$, so $A\pi^\star-B\ge0$ and $\pi'-\pi^\star\ge0$. If $\pi^\star=1$ then $B\ge A$, so $A\pi^\star-B\le0$ and $\pi'-\pi^\star\le0$. In both clipped cases the second term is nonnegative.

Any rule that assigns one weight per cell, such as a per-layer table, a weight shared per noise level, a hard switch, or a table fitted elsewhere, is a feasible $\pi'$ in every cell, so its total error is at least the per-head table's, by $\sum_{\text{cells}}A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2$ or more. A table interpolated in $\sigma$ between bin centres is not constant within a bin; the statement applies to the bin-constant table used to score rules in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The frequency argument in more detail.} Argument~\ref{arg:freq} rests on two facts and one assumption. The first fact is that the noisy latent is a sum of signal and independent white noise, so a band's signal-to-noise ratio scales as $((1-\sigma)/\sigma)^2S(f)$; at $\sigma=0.89$ the factor is $0.015$, and at $\sigma=0.5$ it is 1. The second is that a window of $w$ tokens cannot distinguish a component of period longer than $w$ from a smooth trend within the window, so windows cannot coordinate a layout at that scale except through what earlier layers mixed into their keys. The assumption is that natural-image latent spectra fall with frequency, which we have not measured for FLUX.1 latents. Together they predict that windowed attention fails first and worst at high noise and for objects larger than the window, which matches the duplication we observe (Figure~\ref{fig:failure}) and the effect of a 16-token window.

\section{Calibration details and statistics}
\label{app:calib}

\paragraph{Statistics.} For every emulated scale, block, head, and noise bin, the pass stores $A$, $B$, $E$, the error of the local branch alone $\sum\|\Ofull-\Oloc\|^2$, $\sum\|\Ofull\|^2$, and the query count, in float64, summed over image queries and channels (\texttt{sca/calib.py}). The sampler's trajectory is that of unmodified full attention; the emulated operators only observe. Statistics are written per prompt and merged by summation, so prompts can be added later and split-half fits need no extra pass. Text-query outputs are not in the objective, because \sca{} sends text queries through the global branch only.

\paragraph{Geometry at emulated scale.} At $1024^2$ the grid is $64\times64$. \section{Implementation details and cost}
\label{app:impl}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{\sca{}: calibration (once per model) and one attention layer at sampling time.}
  \label{alg:sca}
  \small
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Statex \textbf{Calibrate}(prompts, $s_c$) on an in-distribution $n\times n$ grid:
    \State sample with full attention; at every block $l$, step $\sigma$, and head $h$:
    \State \quad $\Oglob,\Oloc\gets$ Eqs.~\ref{eq:glob}, \ref{eq:loc} with subsampling $s_c$, window $n/s_c$, tile $tn/(s_cn_0)$
    \State \quad add $\|\Oloc{-}\Oglob\|^2$ and $\langle\Ofull{-}\Oglob,\Oloc{-}\Oglob\rangle$ to $A_{lh\beta(\sigma)}$, $B_{lh\beta(\sigma)}$ \Comment{float64}
    \State \Return $\pi_{lh\beta}\gets\clip(B_{lh\beta}/A_{lh\beta},0,1)$ \Comment{Proposition~\ref{prop:opt}}
    \Statex \textbf{Layer}$(Q,K,V,\sigma)$ at target factors $(s_H,s_W)$:
    \State $\Oglob\gets$ Eq.~\ref{eq:glob} for all queries \Comment{block-centre keys, coarse positions}
    \If{$\sigma>\sigma_g$} \Return $\Oglob$ \Comment{opening phase}
    \EndIf
    \State $\Oloc\gets$ Eq.~\ref{eq:loc} for image queries \Comment{one FlexAttention call}
    \State \Return $[\,\Oglob_{\Tset},\;\pih\,\Oloc+(1-\pih)\,\Oglob_{\Gset}\,]$ \Comment{interpolated table; fp32 mix}
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{FlexAttention window.} Image tokens are reordered tile-major, so that a sparse block is a small spatial tile. Query blocks cover $8\times16$ tokens (128 queries) and key blocks $8\times8$ (64 keys), shifted so that unclamped windows start on a block edge; text keys come first. The mask function states window membership per query-key pair, so the result does not depend on the block layout, only the amount of skipped work does. On GH200, $8\times16$ query blocks with $8\times8$ key blocks were fastest at $2048^2$, $4096^2$, and $2048\times6144$. RoPE, subsampling, both branches, and the mix compile into one graph per layer; the mix is in fp32.

\paragraph{Exactness test.} \texttt{tests/test\_sca.py} (18 CPU tests) checks that the processor in full mode matches the stock FLUX attention; that both branches match a brute-force computation of Equations~\ref{eq:glob} and~\ref{eq:loc}; that the local branch with a full-grid window and the global branch with factor 1 both equal full attention; that calibration returns $\pi=1$ when the local branch is exact; that the gate returns the global branch alone; and that the FlexAttention local branch equals a reference that gathers each tile's key window explicitly, to within one bf16 unit in the last place, with both equally far from an fp32 reference.

\paragraph{Position ids in bf16.} The FLUX.1 pipeline builds image position ids in bf16, which cannot represent every integer above 256, so positions beyond 256 tokens per axis are rounded. This affects every method, including full attention and AReA, at $2048\times6144$ (384 tokens per row). \sca{} takes the grid size from the sampler instead of inferring it from the rounded ids, so its window and subsampling geometry match the true grid.

\paragraph{Non-divisible grids.} When $H/n_0$ is not an integer, the subsampling factor per axis is the divisor of $H$ nearest to $H/n_0$, and the tile side the divisor nearest to $t$; neither case arises at the sizes we evaluate.

\section{Evaluation protocol and metric definitions}
\label{app:metrics}

\paragraph{Prompts and references.} The Aesthetic-4K evaluation split~\citep{zhang2025diffusion4k} has 195 prompts with 4K reference images. Four (boats, Moai, woman and bear, underwater surfer) were used to choose the opening phase and window and are excluded everywhere, leaving 191. The 46-prompt ablation subset is fixed before any ablation was run. HRGen-1K~\citep{rajabi2026area} has 1000 prompts and no references; we use the first 200 \todo{E4: confirm selection rule}. The 48 calibration prompts were written for this purpose and share no prompt with Aesthetic-4K (the largest word-set Jaccard overlap with any evaluation prompt is 0.26).

\paragraph{Preference and alignment metrics.} ImageReward v1.0~\citep{xu2023imagereward} (bicubic resize to 224, centre crop). HPSv2 and HPSv2.1~\citep{wu2023hpsv2}: cosine of normalized image and text features with the official validation transform (longest side 224, padded). PickScore~\citep{kirstain2023pickapic}: $\exp(\text{logit scale})$ times the cosine. CLIP score~\citep{radford2021clip}: $100\max(\cos,0)$ with ViT-L/14. Aesthetic: the LAION improved aesthetic predictor (sac+logos+ava1, linear MSE) on the L2-normalized CLIP ViT-L/14 image embedding~\citep{schuhmann2022aesthetic}.

\paragraph{No-reference quality.} MUSIQ~\citep{ke2021musiq} (pyiqa defaults) on the full image at native resolution, and MUSIQ$_\text{c}$, its mean over four fixed random $1024^2$ crops per image; crop positions depend only on the prompt id, so they are identical across methods. CLIP-IQA~\citep{wang2022clipiqa} and ARNIQA~\citep{agnolucci2024arniqa} (pyiqa defaults) on the image downscaled with antialiased bicubic resampling to a long side of 1024 pixels, the scale both were trained on.

\paragraph{Distribution metrics.} FID~\citep{heusel2017fid} and KID between the 191 generated images and the 195 references, with Inception-v3 features (torch-fidelity) after a bilinear resize to $299^2$. Patch FID and KID use the same four $1024^2$ crops per image on both sides (764 and 780 crops). Inception score uses the full image at $299^2$, 10 splits.

\paragraph{Intervals.} All intervals are 95\% percentile bootstraps over prompts, paired: each draw resamples prompt ids and recomputes both methods' metric on the same ids, then their difference. Main comparisons use 1000 draws, ablations 2000 with seed 0 (\texttt{scripts/paired\_bootstrap.py}). For set-level metrics (FID, KID, their patch versions, Inception score) the bootstrap resamples images within the evaluated set, which biases the absolute value upward because duplicates reduce diversity; the point estimate can therefore lie outside the interval of the absolute value, and for full attention's patch FID it lies just outside the interval of the difference too. We use these intervals only for the sign of differences.

\section{Calibration table at non-square sizes}
\label{app:ps2}

At $(s_H,s_W)=(2,4)$ the two axes have different factors, and \sca{} uses the table of the emulated scale nearest the larger factor, scale 4. On the 46 ablation prompts at $2048\times4096$, the scale-2 table changes the paired metrics negligibly (Table~\ref{tab:app_ps2}): HPSv2 $+0.09$, HPSv2.1 $+0.17$ (both $\times100$), and PickScore $+0.05$ are significant but small, and ImageReward ($+0.01$ $[-0.03,0.05]$) and the other metrics are within noise. \section{The low-band duplication guard}
\label{app:band}

\paragraph{Construction.} Let $\LP$ average-pool a head's image output over $p\times p$ tokens and upsample it bilinearly, and $\HP=\mathrm{I}-\LP$. The guard replaces the low band of the mix $M$ by that of the global branch:
\begin{equation}
  O \;=\; \LP\big(\Oglob\big) + \HP\big(M\big) \;=\; \Oglob + \pih\,\HP\big(\Oloc-\Oglob\big).
\end{equation}
The local branch then contributes only its difference from the global branch above the cutoff. The cutoff should lie below $1/(2s)$, where the global branch aliases, and above $1/w$, below which the window cannot resolve; with $w=64$ and $s=4$ this leaves periods of 8 to 64 tokens. We used $p=16$; $p=8$ and $p=32$ were worse on four development prompts, and a noise-dependent cutoff behaved like $p=16$. FreeScale~\citep{qiu2024freescale} also fuses two self-attention outputs by frequency band, in the opposite direction: it keeps the blurred low band of attention over overlapping crops and takes the high band from full self-attention at the target size. The guard instead takes the low band from the global branch, which stays at native token count and offsets, and keeps the per-head weight on the high band.

\paragraph{History: what it fixes and what it costs.} On the development prompts, the band alone (no opening phase) fixed the boats but left extra statues and faces on the Moai and woman-and-bear prompts; the opening phase alone got the main subject right, but small extra figures appeared late on some prompts; both together gave one subject on the boats, Moai, and woman-and-bear prompts. This was our first full configuration. On 46 prompts, however, removing the guard improves HPSv2 ($+0.54$), HPSv2.1 ($+1.17$), PickScore ($+0.25$), CLIP-IQA ($+1.7$), and MUSIQ ($+2.9$), all significant, leaves the other metrics within noise, and saves 17\,s per image on an idle GPU. The guard also hid what the per-head mix contributes. With it (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_band}), local only matched the per-head mix within 2\% on preference metrics, because the guard replaces the low band of every head's output with that of the global branch whatever the head's weight, so it injects global low frequencies even at $\pi=1$; without the guard local only loses on nearly every metric (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}). Likewise, with the guard one shared weight and per-layer weights matched the per-head table on preference metrics and lost only on no-reference quality, and hard per-head routing (10 to 17\% of heads global at threshold 0.5, about 6\% at 0.3) stayed within 2\% of the mix. The guard is an option for prompts where late duplication is visible, not part of the default method.

\section{Reproducing AReA}
\label{app:area}

We run AReA's released code with its FLUX.1 defaults (block-centre pooling, first phase for 8 of 28 steps, halo width 8, bridge ratio 0.5), the FLUX.1-dev checkpoint, guidance 4.5, 28 steps, $\mu$ at its maximum, and the seeds shared with the other methods. AReA's paper reports ImageReward 1.32 at 4K under its own settings; our shared protocol gives 0.92, so cross-paper numbers are not comparable, while all numbers in this paper come from one protocol. AReA's paper reports 151\,s for AReA and 314\,s for full attention at $4096^2$ on an H100; we measure 212.6\,s and 184.6\,s on one GH200 (medians of three images). We use commit \texttt{9e38ec2} of the released repository, which includes a cost cap on halo and bridge tokens and a jagged halo edge; neither appears in the paper text, and the paper does not say whether its Table~1 used them. Recomputed from the code with 512 text tokens, the first-phase pooling factors are 4, 4, 4, 4, 2, 2, 2, 2 at $4096\times4096$ and $2048\times4096$, and 8, 8, 8, 4, 4, 4, 2, 2 at $2048\times6144$. The second-phase windows shrink over 20 steps from 180, 151, and 192 tokens respectively to 64. At $2048\times6144$ no window is cheaper than full attention once the text tokens are counted, so the first window falls back to 192 tokens and the cap drops the bridge and halo on the first three second-phase steps. Other differences from the release defaults are the FLUX.1-dev checkpoint instead of FLUX.1-Krea-dev, and one seed per prompt shared across methods instead of one seed for all prompts. \todo{E9: AReA with its default FLUX.1-Krea-dev checkpoint, against recalibrated \sca{}.}

\section{Failure cases and limitations}
\label{app:failures}

\paragraph{Late duplication.} With the final method, small extra figures can appear on some prompts in the last 20 steps, when the local branch dominates most heads. The band guard suppresses some of them but costs preference scores. \paragraph{Articulated subjects.} The underwater-surfer development prompt keeps doubled limbs in every variant; AReA's image shows a large translucent ghost. \paragraph{Small factors.} At $2048\times4096$ the window covers half the image height, so the local branch alone already sees most of the image along that axis, and the per-head mix gains little over AReA's shrinking windows; aesthetic score and patch FID are worse. \paragraph{Text-image offsets.} The local branch keeps out-of-range text-image offsets (Remark~\ref{rem:text}). \paragraph{Metrics.} No-reference quality metrics reward sharp repeated subjects (v0 scores higher on CLIP-IQA and ARNIQA), and FID against 195 references at $299^2$ resolution sees little of the 4K detail; we therefore report every metric and the qualitative galleries.

\section{Compute and reproducibility}
\label{app:compute}

\paragraph{Compute.} All runs used single NVIDIA GH200 GPUs (96\,GB) on a shared cluster. Calibration took about 1.5 GPU-hours. Generation at $4096^2$ takes about 71\,s per image for \sca{}, 262\,s for AReA, and 197\,s for full attention on shared nodes (campaign means); the main comparisons, ablations, and non-square sizes comprise more than 2{,}000 generated images. \todo{Lead: total GPU-hours including E1 to E9, from the scheduler accounting.}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} The code (attention processor, calibration, sampling, evaluation wrappers, AReA wrapper, and tests), the calibration table \texttt{k1\_1024.npz}, the per-image metric files, and the per-comparison summary and paired-delta CSV files behind every table will be released. All main-text numbers are generated from those CSV files. Spool tasks ran from frozen copies of the code at a fixed commit, and each image's sidecar records the settings and a code fingerprint.

\section{Broader impact}
\label{app:impact}

\sca{} lowers the cost of generating very large images with existing open-weight models, which helps applications such as print, design, and scientific visualization, and lowers the energy per image relative to full attention. The same reduction lowers the cost of producing high-resolution synthetic images that could be used to mislead. \sca{} adds no capability beyond the base model's and does not change its safety filters or watermarking; the risks and mitigations of the base model apply unchanged.
