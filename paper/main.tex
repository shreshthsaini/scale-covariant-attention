\begin{abstract}
Diffusion transformers trained near one megapixel can generate 4K images without retraining once their attention is adapted. Each such method must set how far every attention head looks, its reach, at a size the model never saw. Current methods share one schedule across heads or search a setting per size. We show that reach is a property of the head: at the training resolution of FLUX.1-dev, the head explains \hlVarHead\% of the variance of reach and the denoising step \hlVarStep\%. We introduce Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}), which mixes a native-size window and a native-size grid over the whole image with one number per head. Reach emulation fits that number once at $1024^2$, where full attention still works, in closed form. Inside HRDiT's sampler, \sca{} makes a $4096^2$ image in \hlSecProg\,s, $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA, and beats AReA on all ten per-image metrics at three seeds. It is statistically indistinguishable from HRDiT on three preference scores in \hlProgHrditPct\% of HRDiT's time. Code: \codeurl.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Diffusion transformers~\citep{peebles2022dit} such as FLUX.1~\citep{flux2024} are trained near one megapixel. At $4096^2$ an image has 16 times as many tokens, full attention costs about 256 times as much, and every layer meets unseen position offsets. Full attention then returns stripes or a flat field (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). Training-free methods therefore change the attention and must decide how far each head looks at the target size: its \emph{reach}. AReA~\citep{rajabi2026area} sees head-averaged attention narrow during denoising and gives all heads one schedule; HRDiT~\citep{xue2026hrdit} searches per-head scopes for each target size; UltraImage~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage} types heads at run time. None of these choices can be checked at the target, where full attention fails. We ask whether reach can be measured where full attention works, at the training resolution, and reused at any size.

At the training resolution, reach is a property of the head (Figure~\ref{fig:observation}). Over 32 prompts at $1024^2$, we record each of FLUX.1-dev's 1,368 heads' share of image attention within 16 tokens of the query at every noise level after the first 8 steps. The head explains \hlVarHead\% of the variance of this share and the noise level \hlVarStep\%. The curve shared by all heads, all that one schedule for every head can follow, explains \hlVarAvg\%. UltraImage noted that heads keep stable local and global roles~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage}; we give the first decomposition of reach variance into heads and steps.

We introduce Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}; Figure~\ref{fig:method}). Each head mixes a native-size window around the query, for detail, and a native-size grid of keys over the whole image, for layout, with one number: its reach $\pi_h\in[0,1]$. \emph{Reach emulation} fits $\pi_h$ at $1024^2$: shrinking both views by the target's image-to-window ratio $r$ reproduces the target's proportions, and the model's own full attention there is a valid reference. The best weight has a closed form, and one pass fills a \emph{reach table} for every size; the table repeats on disjoint prompt halves (correlation \hlEmuSplitHalfTwo). To our knowledge, no prior method measures per-head reach once at the training resolution and reuses it at any size.

Inside HRDiT's three-stage sampler, progressive \sca{} makes a $4096^2$ FLUX.1-dev image in \hlSecProg\,s on one GH200, $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA and $\hlSpeedProgHrdit\times$ faster than HRDiT (Figure~\ref{fig:prog_hero}). It beats AReA on all ten per-image metrics at each of three seeds and on HRGen-1K, with ImageReward rising from \hlAreaIrAbs{} to \hlProgIrAbs{} ($\hlProgAreaIr$ $[\hlProgAreaIrLo,\hlProgAreaIrHi]$, $n=\nmain$, seed 0). Against HRDiT, its ImageReward, HPSv2.1, and PickScore are statistically indistinguishable (every 95\% interval includes zero) in \hlProgHrditPct\% of HRDiT's time. A single-pass mode is $\hlSpeedArea\times$ faster than AReA and keeps the most crop-level detail; the progressive schedule costs patch-level fidelity, where HRDiT and single-pass \sca{} lead.

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=0pt]
  \item We show that reach is a property of the head: at the training resolution, the head explains \hlVarHead\% to \hlVarHeadFluxTwo\% of its variance in three FLUX models (Section~\ref{sec:observation}).
  \item We introduce \sca{}, which mixes a native-size window and grid with one weight per head, and reach emulation, which fits that weight once at $1024^2$ in closed form (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item We show that progressive \sca{} makes a $4096^2$ image in \hlSecProg\,s, $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA and better on all ten per-image metrics at three seeds, and that per-head weights carry the gain (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Reach is a property of the head}
\label{sec:observation}\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Reach.} FLUX.1 applies joint attention to text and image tokens in 57 blocks of 24 heads and is trained near $1024^2$ pixels, a grid of $64\times64$ image tokens. For one head and a query $i$, split the keys into a near set around the query and a far set. The output splits exactly as $\Ofull_i=m_i\,O^{\mathrm{near}}_i+(1-m_i)\,O^{\mathrm{far}}_i$, where $O^{\mathrm{near}}_i$ and $O^{\mathrm{far}}_i$ attend only to their own set and $m_i$ is the near set's softmax mass (Lemma~\ref{lem:split}). This near share is the head's reach at that query: near 1 for a head that looks near, near 0 for one that looks far (Figure~\ref{fig:observation}a). We record it within 16 tokens for 256 seeded queries per block and step, over 32 purpose-written prompts at $1024^2$ outside the evaluation sets\fullonly{ (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts})}.

\paragraph{The head sets the reach.} After the first 8 steps (noise level $\sigma<0.89$), the head explains \hlVarHead\% of the variance of this share over all heads and noise levels, and the noise level within a head \hlVarStep\% (Figure~\ref{fig:observation}b,c). The head-averaged curve rises from \hlVarCurveLo{} to \hlVarCurveHi{}, the narrowing AReA reports, yet explains only \hlVarAvg\%; no schedule shared by all heads can explain more. FLUX.1-Krea-dev~\citep{krea2025flux} and FLUX.2-dev~\citep{flux2_2025} give \hlVarHeadKrea\% and \hlVarHeadFluxTwo\% for the head (\shortalt{Table~\ref{tab:variance_split}}{Figure~\ref{fig:observation}c}). One number per head thus captures nearly all of the variation and, because it barely moves with the step, can be measured once.

\section{Scale-Covariant Attention}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Two native-size views}
\label{sec:setting}\label{sec:branches}

On its native grid the model saw position offsets of at most 63 tokens per axis and $64^2$ image keys per query. \sca{} keeps both at any size with two views: a near view $\Oloc_i$, a native-size window around the query, and a far view $\Oglob_i$, a native-size grid over the whole image. An image query $i$ of head $h$ mixes them as $O_i=\pi_h\Oloc_i+(1-\pi_h)\Oglob_i$, with the head's reach $\pi_h\in[0,1]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:method}c).

\paragraph{Setting.} A multimodal diffusion transformer such as FLUX.1 concatenates $T$ text tokens $\Tset$ with $N=HW$ image tokens on the grid $\Gset=[H]\times[W]$, where $[n]=\{0,\dots,n-1\}$. At $1024^2$ the grid is $n_0\times n_0$ with $n_0=64$. Rotary embeddings~\citep{su2021roformer} give token $j$ a position $p_j\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ (text tokens at $0$) and a rotation $R_p$ with $R_p^\top R_{p'}=R_{p'-p}$. For one head, a pre-rotation query $q$ placed at $u$, and a set $\Kset$ of (key, value, key position) triples, attention is
\begin{equation}
  \Attn(q,u;\Kset) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{(k,v,u')\in\Kset} \operatorname{softmax}_{\Kset}\!\big(q^\top R_{u'-u}\,k/\sqrt d\big)\; v ,
  \label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the head dimension. Full attention $\Ofull_i$ attends to all keys at their own positions; at a target grid $H=s_Hn_0$, $W=s_Wn_0$ ($s$ when equal) it meets offsets up to $s$ times the trained range.

\paragraph{Near view.} Image queries form tiles of $t\times t$ tokens ($t=8$), and the window $\Win_w(i)$ of query $i$ is the $w\times w$ block centred on its tile and clamped to the grid (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}). Its keys are the text tokens, a bridge set $\mathcal{B}$ of image tokens, and the window, at their original positions:
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i(w)=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_j,v_j,p_j)\}_{j\in\mathcal{B}\cup\Win_w(i)},\qquad
  \Oloc_i(w)=\Attn\big(q_i,\,p_i;\,\Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i(w)\big),
  \label{eq:loc}
\end{equation}
where $\Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i(w)$ is the local key set; one FlexAttention call computes it~\citep{dong2024flex}. The bridge lets each window see the whole image. In each cell $c$ of an $8\times8$ partition, it keeps the 16 tokens whose pre-rotation keys lie farthest from the mean image key (AReA's rule;~\citealp{rajabi2026area}):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{B}=\textstyle\bigcup_{c}\operatorname{top}_{16}\big\{\sum_{h}\|k^{h}_j-\bar k^{h}\|^2 : j\in c\big\},
  \label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
where $\bar k^{h}$ is the mean image key of head $h$. With $w=n_0$ the window is an exact native-size sliding window, for content of fixed token size, such as edges and texture.

\paragraph{Far view.} Let $\Gnat=[H/s_H]\times[W/s_W]$ be the $n_0\times n_0$ grid of blocks. Block-centre subsampling keeps for each block $b$ the token at $\kappa(b)=(s_Hb_1+\lfloor s_H/2\rfloor,\,s_Wb_2+\lfloor s_W/2\rfloor)$, and $c(p)=(\lfloor p_1/s_H\rfloor,\lfloor p_2/s_W\rfloor)$ maps a position to its block:
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_{\kappa(b)},v_{\kappa(b)},b)\}_{b\in\Gnat},\qquad
  \Oglob_i=\Attn\big(q_i,\,c(p_i);\,\Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}\big),
  \label{eq:glob}
\end{equation}
where text queries sit at $c(p_i)=0$. This is AReA's first phase at pooling factor $s$, for content that spans a fixed fraction of the image, such as layout. Every offset of the grid and of the native window stays in the trained range, and the cost is linear in $N$ (Propositions~\ref{prop:range} and~\ref{prop:cost}). At $4096^2$, \sca{} scores \hlWorkedPairsRatio{} times fewer query-key pairs than full attention (\shortalt{Table~\ref{tab:flops}}{full version}).

\subsection{Reach emulation}
\label{sec:emulation}

Full attention fails at the target, so nothing there can score $\pi_h$; reach emulation scores it on a miniature of the target at $1024^2$, against the model's own full attention (Figure~\ref{fig:method}a). On the native $n\times n$ grid ($n=64$), emulated ratio $r$ uses subsampling factor $r$, window side $n/r$, and tile side $\mathrm{round}(tn/(rn_0))$, so the image-to-window ratio is $r$, as at a target $r$ times larger. The sampler runs unmodified full attention (Algorithm~\ref{alg:emulate}).

\paragraph{Closed-form fit.} For block $l$, head $h$, noise bin $\beta$, and ratio $r$, we accumulate three sums over prompts, steps with $\sigma\in\beta$, image queries, and channels:
\begin{equation}
  A=\textstyle\sum\|\Oloc-\Oglob\|^2,\quad
  B=\sum\langle \Ofull-\Oglob,\,\Oloc-\Oglob\rangle,\quad
  E=\sum\|\Ofull-\Oglob\|^2,
  \label{eq:stats}
\end{equation}
where $\Oloc$ and $\Oglob$ are the emulated views without the bridge. A weight $\pi$ then has squared error $J(\pi)=E-2\pi B+\pi^2A$.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form fit and transfer cost]
\label{prop:opt}
In each cell $(l,h,\beta,r)$, $J$ is minimized on $[0,1]$ by $\pi^\star=\clip(B/A,0,1)$, with $\pi^\star=1$ if $A=0$. Any other weight $\pi'\in[0,1]$ constant in the cell has $J(\pi')-J(\pi^\star)\ge A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2$, with equality when $0<\pi^\star<1$. This covers weights fitted at another ratio or resolution, per layer, or shared, and a hard switch.
\end{proposition}
One pass collects ratios 2 and 4 and fills the reach table $\pi_{lh}(\sigma;r)$, and its sums score every other rule without another pass. We assume that the weight depends only on $r$, which is scale covariance.

\paragraph{One table serves every size.} Tables fitted on disjoint prompt halves correlate at \hlEmuSplitHalfTwo{} at ratio 2 and \hlEmuSplitHalfFour{} at ratio 4 (Figure~\ref{fig:emulation}). In relative output error, $\sum J/\sum\|\Ofull\|^2$, the ratio-2 table applied at ratio 4 scores \hlEmuXferRatio{} against \hlEmuErrHeadFour{} for the ratio-4 table. The $1024^2$ table scored at $1280^2$ gives \hlEmuXferRes{} against \hlEmuOwnRes, although the two resolutions emulate ratio 2 with windows of 32 and 40 tokens. The weights depend on the ratio, as scale covariance assumes.

\paragraph{Single-pass mode.} \sca{} also samples at the target in one pass of 28 steps. A window schedule $w(\sigma)$ sets the ratio $r(\sigma)=\max(H,W)/w(\sigma)$, and an image query takes
\begin{equation}
  O_i \;=\; \pi\,\Oloc_i\big(w(\sigma)\big) + (1-\pi)\,\Oglob_i \;\text{ if } \sigma\le\sigma_g, \qquad O_i=\Oglob_i \;\text{ if } \sigma>\sigma_g,
  \label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
where $\pi=\pi_{lh}(\sigma;r(\sigma))$, $\sigma_g=0.89$, and text queries take $\Oglob_i$. Between ratios 2 and 4, $\pi$ is linear in $\log r$, and in $\sigma$ it is interpolated between occupied noise bins:
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{lh}(\sigma;r)=(1-\lambda)\,\pi_{lh}(\sigma;2)+\lambda\,\pi_{lh}(\sigma;4),\qquad \lambda=\clip(\log_2 r-1,\,0,\,1),
  \label{eq:sched}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the weight of the ratio-4 table. The global view runs alone for the first 8 of 28 steps, as in AReA, because output error cannot see layout (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}). Windows of 128, 112, 96, and 80 tokens then take one step each before the native $w=n_0$. The gate, the window sizes, and the 1,024 bridge tokens are set by hand; only the weights are measured (\shortalt{Table~\ref{tab:handset}}{full version}). With $w=n_0$ right after the gate and no bridge, the operator is \sca{} without the schedule.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
Training-free high-resolution methods alter convolutions, spectra, or attention temperature~\citep{he2023scalecrafter,zhang2023hidiffusion,huang2024fouriscale,jin2023training}, fuse global and patch paths~\citep{du2023demofusion,yang2024fam} or split them by frequency~\citep{qiu2024freescale}, add native-resolution guidance~\citep{du2024imax,bu2025hiflow,koh2025scalediff}, or adapt RoPE~(\citealp{zhuo2024lumina,issachar2025dype,zhao2026sigma,rajabi2026sega}; \mbox{\citealp{liu2026tide}}). \sca{} borrows AReA's opening, block-centre keys, and bridge selection\fullonly{ (Table~\ref{tab:related})}, and ResDiT also mixes position-scaled global and native-size patch attention~\citep{ma2025resdit}. Besides HRDiT and UltraImage (Section~\ref{sec:intro}), sparse attention varies windows, spans, or key scales per layer or head in diffusion transformers~\citep{yuan2024ditfastattn,xi2025svg,zhang2025sta}, language models~\citep{fu2024moa,xiao2024duoattention}, and vision transformers~\citep{pan2022hilo}. These settings are fitted against dense attention at the size where they run, which at $4096^2$ is the failure. Head roles, two branches (our local branch is neighbourhood attention;~\citealp{hassani2022nat}), a global opening, and shrinking windows have precedents.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}
\paragraph{Limitations.} Progressive \sca{}'s patch FID is higher than HRDiT's and single-pass \sca{}'s, and inside its sampler coarser weights have lower patch FID than per-head weights. In single-pass mode, small additional figures occasionally appear in scenes with people or animals; the progressive configuration reduces this to the level of the multi-stage baselines. The opening, window sizes, and bridge are set by hand, and the window sizes were chosen on the 46-prompt subset, part of the evaluation set. Progressive \sca{} depends on HRDiT's sampler, which does not support FLUX.1-Krea-dev, so the $6144^2$, $8192^2$, FLUX.2-dev, and sensitivity results use \sca{} without the schedule. Reach emulation omits the bridge and sees 16 times fewer keys per view than the target, so the deployed operator's output error is untested. All metrics are automatic.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Reach is a property of the head, so \sca{} fits it once per head at the training resolution, where full attention is still correct, and reuses it at any size. Inside HRDiT's sampler it is $\hlSpeedProgArea\times$ faster than AReA at $4096^2$, better on all ten per-image metrics at three seeds, and statistically indistinguishable from HRDiT on three preference scores in \hlProgHrditPct\% of HRDiT's time\ifanynewmodel; it also runs on \newmodelList\fi.
