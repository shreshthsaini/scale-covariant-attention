\begin{abstract}
Diffusion transformers trained at one megapixel fail at 4K: full attention meets unfamiliar token counts and position offsets, and its cost grows quadratically. Training-free fixes edit positional encodings or restrict the keys each query sees; AReA, the strongest baseline under our protocol, applies one key schedule to every head. We find that each head's balance between local detail and global layout is stable and can be measured at the training resolution. Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}) emulates a larger target inside the training distribution, fits one local/global mixing weight per head and noise level in closed form against the model's own full attention, and reuses the resulting table at the target size. Block-centre global attention and an exact native-size sliding window give every image query the native number of keys at linear cost, and a short global-only opening fixes the layout. On 191 Aesthetic-4K prompts at $4096^2$ with FLUX.1-dev, \sca{} raises ImageReward over AReA from 0.92 to 1.23 and HPSv2.1 from 27.2 to 29.7 while cutting generation time from 202 to 71 seconds on a GH200, and it also beats full attention, DyPE, and SEGA under the same sampler. Speedups over AReA reach $2.99\times$ at $6144^2$ and $3.31\times$ at $8192^2$. UltraImage's costlier two-stage recipe keeps higher preference scores, while \sca{} scores higher on CLIP and crop MUSIQ. Gains over AReA hold on HRGen-1K and two more seeds, \sca{} transfers to FLUX.2-dev, and results at $2048\times4096$ are mixed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Diffusion transformers~\citep{peebles2022dit} such as FLUX.1~\citep{flux2024} and Stable Diffusion~3~\citep{esser2024sd3} are trained at about one megapixel. At $4096\times4096$ the image-token count grows 16-fold and full-attention cost about 256-fold, and every attention layer sees token counts and position offsets outside its training distribution. Outputs show duplicated subjects, broken layouts, and blurred texture~\citep{he2023scalecrafter,du2023demofusion,issachar2025dype,rajabi2026area}; in FLUX.1-dev at $4096^2$, full attention collapses into stripes or a flat field (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}).

Training-free fixes edit the positional encoding~\citep{zhuo2024lumina,issachar2025dype,zhao2025ultraimage,rajabi2026sega} or restrict the keys each query sees~\citep{zhang2023hidiffusion,ma2025resdit,xue2026hrdit,rajabi2026area}. AReA~\citep{rajabi2026area}, the most recent of the second kind, attends to keys subsampled onto a native-size grid for the first 8 of 28 steps, then to windows that shrink to native size, and shares this schedule across heads. Head-specific receptive fields are also established: HRDiT assigns per-head scopes under a cost budget~\citep{xue2026hrdit}, and UltraImage concentrates attention per head using entropy~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage}. We ask a narrower question: can a head's local/global output mixture be fitted at native resolution and transferred to larger images?

We answer it by emulation. To mimic a target $s$ times larger, we shrink both a native-size neighbourhood and a downsampled view of the whole image by $s$, so the image-to-window ratio matches the target while full attention stays in distribution and serves as ground truth. A convex mix of the two views fits each head well. The fitted weight differs strongly between heads, is stable across tested prompt splits (correlation 0.99), and transfers: the table fitted at emulated scale 2 explains scale 4 with 2.4\% more output error than the scale-4 table, and a $1024^2$ table explains $1280^2$ with 0.8\% more (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). We call this measured transfer scale covariance: the local view follows a law fixed in tokens, the global view a law fixed in image fractions, and over the tested scales the weight between them stays nearly constant.

Scale-Covariant Attention (\sca{}) turns this measurement into an attention operator (Figure~\ref{fig:method}). Each head uses its calibrated weight to mix two branches: a global branch that attends to block-centre keys on the native grid with rescaled coordinates, and a local branch that is an exact native-size sliding window. The calibrated mix alone does not preserve layout: applied from the first step at $4096^2$, it draws five Moai statues for a prompt asking for one (Figure~\ref{fig:failure}). \sca{} therefore runs the global branch alone for the first 8 of 28 steps (the opening phase), as a frequency argument suggests (Argument~\ref{arg:freq}). Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Calibration by emulation.} A closed-form procedure that emulates the target scale inside the training distribution and fits each head's receptive-field mix to the model's own full attention from one pass of statistics (Proposition~\ref{prop:opt}). On FLUX.1-dev the mix is head-specific, prompt-stable, and transfers across scale and calibration resolution.
  \item \textbf{The \sca{} operator.} A per-head, per-noise-level mix of two branches whose image query-key offsets stay in the trained range (Proposition~\ref{prop:range}) at a cost linear in the token count (Proposition~\ref{prop:cost}). One attention layer at $4096^2$ takes 18.7\,ms, full attention 87.3\,ms.
  \item \textbf{Paired evidence against five baselines.} Full attention, AReA, DyPE, and SEGA under one FLUX.1-dev protocol and UltraImage under its own recipe, with paired bootstrap intervals, ablations, failure galleries, and checks on HRGen-1K, extra seeds, and FLUX.2-dev. At $4096^2$, \sca{} beats AReA on every preference, alignment, and no-reference quality metric and runs $2.85\times$ faster.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scale-Covariant Attention}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setting and notation}
\label{sec:setting}
A multimodal diffusion transformer such as FLUX.1 concatenates $T$ text tokens $\Tset$ with $N=HW$ image tokens on the grid $\Gset=[H]\times[W]$, $[n]=\{0,\dots,n-1\}$, and applies joint attention in each block (57 blocks of 24 heads in FLUX.1). It is trained at about $1024^2$ pixels, a native grid of $n_0\times n_0$ tokens with $n_0=64$. Rotary embeddings~\citep{su2021roformer} give token $j$ a position $p_j\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ (FLUX.1 puts all text tokens at $0$) and a rotation $R_p$ with $R_p^\top R_{p'}=R_{p'-p}$. For one head, a pre-rotation query $q$ placed at $u$, and a set $\Kset$ of (key, value, key position) triples,
\begin{equation}
  \Attn(q,u;\Kset) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{(k,v,u')\in\Kset} \operatorname{softmax}_{\Kset}\!\big(q^\top R_{u'-u}\,k/\sqrt d\big)\; v .
  \label{eq:attn}
\end{equation}
Full attention is $\Ofull_i=\Attn\big(q_i,p_i;\{(k_j,v_j,p_j)\}_{j\in\Tset\cup\Gset}\big)$. The target grid is $H=s_H n_0$, $W=s_W n_0$ with integer factors ($s$ when equal), so full attention meets offsets $s$ times the trained range. The latent at noise level $\sigma$ is $x_\sigma=(1-\sigma)x_0+\sigma\epsilon$. Splitting the keys into a near and a far set gives exactly $\Ofull_i=m_iO^{\mathrm{near}}_i+(1-m_i)O^{\mathrm{far}}_i$, where $O^{\mathrm{near}}_i$ and $O^{\mathrm{far}}_i$ attend only to their own set and $m_i$ is the softmax mass on the near set (Lemma~\ref{lem:split}): every head is a query-dependent mix of a near and a far field. \sca{} approximates this mix with two separately normalized operators and one weight per head. Because both branches include text keys and their image key sets can overlap, the identity does not decompose \sca{} exactly.

\subsection{Two operators with native-range image geometry}
\label{sec:branches}

\paragraph{Global operator.} Let $\Gnat=[H/s_H]\times[W/s_W]$ be the $n_0\times n_0$ grid of blocks. Block-centre subsampling keeps for each block $b$ the token at $\kappa(b)=(s_H b_1+\lfloor s_H/2\rfloor,\, s_W b_2+\lfloor s_W/2\rfloor)$, and the coarse map $c(p)=(\lfloor p_1/s_H\rfloor,\lfloor p_2/s_W\rfloor)$ sends a position to its block, so $c(\kappa(b))=b$:
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_{\kappa(b)},v_{\kappa(b)},b)\}_{b\in\Gnat},\qquad
  \Oglob_i=\Attn\big(q_i,\,c(p_i);\,\Kset^{\mathrm{glob}}\big),
  \label{eq:glob}
\end{equation}
with $c(p_i)$ replaced by 0 for text queries. This is AReA's first phase with the pooling factor fixed at $s$. It models content whose extent is a fixed fraction of the image: layout, object count, identity.

\paragraph{Local operator.} Image queries form tiles of $t\times t$ tokens ($t=8$); query $i$ lies in tile $\tau=\lfloor p_i/t\rfloor$. With window side $w=\min(n_0,H)$, the window of tile $\tau$ starts at $a(\tau)=\clip\big(t\tau+\lfloor t/2\rfloor-\lfloor w/2\rfloor,0,H-w\big)$ per axis, centred on the tile and clamped to the grid. With $\Win(i)$ the $w\times w$ window of $i$'s tile,
\begin{equation}
  \Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i=\{(k_j,v_j,0)\}_{j\in\Tset}\cup\{(k_j,v_j,p_j)\}_{p_j\in\Win(i)},\qquad
  \Oloc_i=\Attn\big(q_i,\,p_i;\,\Kset^{\mathrm{loc}}_i\big),
  \label{eq:loc}
\end{equation}
an exact sliding window with the original positions. It models content whose extent is fixed in tokens: edges, texture, fine structure.

\begin{proposition}[Trained position range]
\label{prop:range}
Assume exact integer position coordinates and $t\le n_0\le\min(H,W)$. Every query-key offset of the global operator and every image query-key offset of the local operator is at most $n_0-1$ per axis, and each image query sees $T+n_0^2$ keys in either operator, as in full attention on the native grid.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Cost]
\label{prop:cost}
Per head and layer, full attention scores $(N+T)^2$ query-key pairs, the global operator $(N+T)(T+N_0)$ with $N_0=|\Gnat|=n_0^2$, and the local operator $N(T+w^2)$. For fixed $n_0$ and $T$ the total is $O\big(N(T+w^2+N_0)\big)$, linear in $N$.
\end{proposition}
Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. Proposition~\ref{prop:range} assumes exact coordinates; the frozen FLUX.1 implementation rounds positions in bf16 beyond 256 tokens per axis (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}). Native-range geometry also does not make hidden states in-distribution, and text-image offsets in the local operator equal $p_i$, as in full attention at the target (Remark~\ref{rem:text}). At $4096^2$ \sca{} scores 7.2 times fewer query-key pairs than full attention, 8.4 times fewer with the opening phase (Table~\ref{tab:flops}).

\subsection{Per-head mixing and the opening phase}
\label{sec:mix}
For an image query of head $h$ in block $l$ at noise level $\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
  O_i \;=\; \Oglob_i \;\;\text{if } \sigma>\sigma_g,\qquad
  O_i \;=\; \pi_{lh}(\sigma)\,\Oloc_i + \big(1-\pi_{lh}(\sigma)\big)\,\Oglob_i \;\;\text{otherwise},
  \label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
and text queries use $\Oglob_i$. The weight $\pi_{lh}(\sigma)$, written $\pih$, stands in for the near-field mass $m_i$; one weight per head is cheap and a few prompts determine it.

\begin{argument}[A frequency-based explanation for the opening phase]
\label{arg:freq}
At noise level $\sigma$ a spatial frequency band has signal-to-noise ratio $\big((1-\sigma)/\sigma\big)^2 S(f)$ for image spectrum $S$. If latent power falls with frequency, only low frequencies carry signal at high noise, and a window of $w$ tokens spans less than one period of any frequency below $1/w$. Independently attended windows may then repeat local structures instead of forming one large object, while a global branch supplies context across windows. Hidden states also carry information from earlier layers, so this is a mechanistic hypothesis, not an impossibility result (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
\end{argument}
Output matching cannot see this failure: a tokenwise squared error can favour local detail without preserving layout. We have not measured per-band signal-to-noise ratios of FLUX.1 latents, but a 16-token window gives smaller and more numerous copies than a 64-token one, as predicted (Figure~\ref{fig:failure}). We set $\sigma_g=0.89$ (the first 8 of 28 steps at maximum shift, as in AReA); gating 13 steps gives a $4\times4$ block mosaic, because all queries of a block share one coarse position.

\subsection{Calibration by output matching}
\label{sec:calib}
The weights must come from where the model is trustworthy, which the target is not. On an in-distribution $n\times n$ grid ($n=64$ at $1024^2$), emulated scale $s_c$ uses subsampling factor $s_c$, window side $n/s_c$, and tile side $\mathrm{round}(tn/(s_cn_0))$, so that the ratio of grid to window is $s_c$, as at a target of scale $s_c$. The sampler runs unmodified full attention; at every block and step both emulated operators are evaluated on the same queries, keys, and values. For block $l$, head $h$, and noise bin $\beta$, sums over prompts, steps with $\sigma\in\beta$, image queries, and channels give
\begin{equation}
  A=\textstyle\sum\|\Oloc-\Oglob\|^2,\quad
  B=\sum\langle \Ofull-\Oglob,\,\Oloc-\Oglob\rangle,\quad
  E=\sum\|\Ofull-\Oglob\|^2,
  \label{eq:stats}
\end{equation}
and the squared error of the mix with weight $\pi$ is $J(\pi)=E-2\pi B+\pi^2A$.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form calibration and transfer cost]
\label{prop:opt}
In each cell $(l,h,\beta)$, $J$ is minimized on $[0,1]$ by $\pi^\star=\clip(B/A,0,1)$ ($\pi^\star=1$ if $A=0$). Any other weight $\pi'\in[0,1]$ (fitted at another scale or resolution, per layer, shared, or a hard switch) has $J(\pi')-J(\pi^\star)\ge A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2$, with equality when $0<\pi^\star<1$.
\end{proposition}
So the per-head table has the lowest output error of any rule constant within a cell, and replacing it costs an $A$-weighted squared distance. This holds at the emulation scale; Section~\ref{sec:analysis} measures how well tables transfer between scales and resolutions. The same sums score every alternative rule without another forward pass. We use 14 noise bins of width $1/14$ and interpolate linearly between the mean noise levels of occupied bins at inference. One pass collects emulated scales 2 and 4. A target with factors $(s_H,s_W)$ uses the table whose scale is nearest $\max(s_H,s_W)$ in log space, here always 4. The local operator is one FlexAttention call~\citep{dong2024flex} over tile-major tokens with the window as an exact block mask (Algorithm~\ref{alg:sca}, Appendix~\ref{app:impl}).

\section{What the heads want}
\label{sec:analysis}

We calibrate FLUX.1-dev on 32 prompts at $1024^2$ and 16 at $1280^2$, written for this purpose and disjoint from the evaluation sets. Errors are relative, $\sum J/\sum\|\Ofull\|^2$, pooled over blocks, heads, and steps.


\paragraph{Heads differ.} Over the 1368 heads, the standard deviation of $\pih$ is 0.24 to 0.26 at every noise level (emulated scale 2). Half of the (head, noise level) cells lie in $[0.2,0.8]$, 11.7\% exceed 0.95, and 2.9\% are below 0.05 (Figure~\ref{fig:method}a; full tables in Figure~\ref{fig:calib}, Appendix~\ref{app:calib}). The mean weight is 0.64 to 0.74, so the typical head leans local but still draws on the global branch.

\paragraph{The table is stable across tested prompt splits.} Tables fitted on disjoint halves of the prompts correlate at 0.989 over all cells (0.986 at scale 4; 0.974 for 16 prompts at $1280^2$), and each half's table scored on the other half gives a relative error of 0.0576 against 0.0574 in sample. Tables from 4, 8, or 16 prompts also generate similarly (Section~\ref{sec:generality}).

\paragraph{The weights transfer across the tested scales and resolutions.} The scale-2 table applied at scale 4 gives a relative error of 0.205 against 0.200 for the scale-4 table, still below every shared rule fitted at scale 4. The $1024^2$ table scored at $1280^2$ gives 0.0589 against 0.0585. Over all pairs of calibration targets, a transferred table adds at most 5.45\% error, while each target's own per-layer fit adds 12 to 24\%. The two resolutions emulate scale 2 with windows of 32 and 40 tokens, so this transfer is consistent with weights that depend mainly on the image-to-window ratio.

\paragraph{Per-head beats coarser rules; output error misses layout.} Relative to the best hard switch, per-head mixing lowers the relative error from 0.092 to 0.057 at scale 2 and from 0.261 to 0.200 at scale 4; per-layer tables reach 0.071 and 0.223, one shared weight per noise level 0.077 and 0.230 (Figure~\ref{fig:relerr}). In output energy the best hard switch never switches to global (Appendix~\ref{app:calib}), so the opening phase deliberately overrides the fit (Argument~\ref{arg:freq}).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Protocol.} All FLUX.1-dev comparisons share AReA's sampler: 28 Euler flow-matching steps, guidance 4.5, shift $\mu=1.15$ at every size, bf16, tiled VAE decoding, and seed label 0 for every prompt. The main prompts are the Aesthetic-4K evaluation split with 4K references~\citep{zhang2025diffusion4k}: 191 prompts after excluding four development prompts, with a fixed 46-prompt subset for ablations. Sizes, written height$\times$width, are $4096^2$, $2048\times4096$, and $2048\times6144$ (191 prompts each), $6144^2$ (100 prompts, \todo{E2}), and $8192^2$ (12 exploratory prompts). Baselines are full attention, AReA from its released code (Appendix~\ref{app:area}), DyPE~\citep{issachar2025dype}, SEGA~\citep{rajabi2026sega}, and UltraImage~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage}. UltraImage runs its released guided two-stage recipe (30 steps per stage, guidance 3.5 then 7), a system-level comparison with a larger sampling budget. We report preference and alignment scores (ImageReward, HPSv2/2.1, PickScore, CLIP, aesthetic; \citealp{xu2023imagereward,wu2023hpsv2,kirstain2023pickapic,radford2021clip,schuhmann2022aesthetic}), distribution scores (FID, KID, patch FID/KID; \citealp{heusel2017fid}), and no-reference quality (MUSIQ on the full image and on $1024^2$ crops, MUSIQ$_\text{c}$; CLIP-IQA; ARNIQA; \citealp{ke2021musiq,wang2022clipiqa,agnolucci2024arniqa}), with 95\% paired bootstrap intervals over prompts. \sca{} uses $t=8$, $w=64$, $\sigma_g=0.89$, and the scale-4 table from 32 prompts at $1024^2$. Each process runs on one NVIDIA GH200 120GB. Table~\ref{tab:protocol} and Appendices~\ref{app:hparams} and~\ref{app:metrics} give every setting and metric definition.

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:main}
\paragraph{Against AReA.} At $4096^2$, \sca{} beats AReA on every preference, alignment, and no-reference quality metric (Table~\ref{tab:main}): ImageReward $+0.31$ $[0.23,0.39]$, HPSv2.1 $+2.50$ $[2.14,2.91]$, PickScore $+0.73$ $[0.62,0.85]$, CLIP $+1.45$ $[1.10,1.81]$, and MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ $+10.9$ $[9.5,12.3]$, from 2000 paired prompt bootstrap draws (Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_quality}, Appendix~\ref{app:phase2}). FID falls by 7.6 $[2.9,12.8]$ under a 1000-draw reference-image-cluster bootstrap; corrected patch FID, patch KID, whole-image KID, and Inception score have intervals spanning zero (Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}). \sca{} takes 71.1\,s per image, a $2.6\times$ speedup over full attention and $2.85\times$ over AReA. Full attention collapses into stripes and is significantly worse than AReA on every reported per-image metric. Crops in Figures~\ref{fig:teaser} and~\ref{fig:qual_main} show the baselines' smoothing, speckle, and tiling.

\paragraph{Other baselines.} Under the shared protocol, \sca{} also improves every reported per-image preference, alignment, and quality metric over DyPE and SEGA (Figure~\ref{fig:baseline_quality}). This comparison fixes the FLUX.1-dev backbone and sampler; it does not reproduce each baseline's separately tuned defaults. UltraImage's two-stage recipe is higher on HPSv2.1 by 1.10 $[0.80,1.40]$ and PickScore by 0.24 $[0.14,0.33]$, and its ImageReward advantage of 0.047 $[-0.016,0.111]$ is unresolved. \sca{} is higher on CLIP by 0.31 $[0.01,0.63]$ and MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ by 19.4 $[18.2,20.7]$, with lower FID and patch FID. \section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Training-free high resolution.} Earlier methods change convolutions, spectra, or attention temperature~\citep{he2023scalecrafter,zhang2023hidiffusion,huang2024fouriscale,jin2023training}, fuse global and patch paths~\citep{du2023demofusion,yang2024fam,qiu2024freescale}, or guide with a native-resolution pass~\citep{du2024imax,bu2025hiflow,koh2025scalediff}. Positional methods adapt RoPE for extrapolation~\citep{zhuo2024lumina,issachar2025dype,zhao2026sigma,rajabi2026sega,liu2026tide}. Closest to \sca{} are methods that split attention into local and global parts. AReA shares one schedule of pooled global attention and shrinking windows across heads~\citep{rajabi2026area}. ResDiT combines position-scaled global attention with native-size patch attention through patch-wise spectral fusion, with global-only, fused, and local-only sampling phases~\citep{ma2025resdit}. FreeScale fuses attention outputs by frequency band~\citep{qiu2024freescale}. HRDiT chooses per-head scopes from an image-loss sensitivity estimate and a cost-constrained integer program~\citep{xue2026hrdit}, and UltraImage combines dominant-frequency correction with entropy-derived per-head attention concentration~\citep{zhao2025ultraimage}; its observation of stable local/global head roles precedes ours. Head specialization, two branches, and a global opening phase are therefore established ingredients. \sca{} adds a specific calibration procedure: emulate the target scale at native resolution, fit a per-head, per-noise-level output mixture in closed form, and transfer it to an operator with native key counts.

\paragraph{Efficient and head-specific attention.} ToDo attends to downsampled keys without rescaling coordinates~\citep{smith2024todo}. DiTFastAttn, Sparse VideoGen, Sliding Tile Attention, and Sparse-vDiT choose windows or sparsity per layer or head at trained resolutions~\citep{yuan2024ditfastattn,xi2025svg,zhang2025sta,chen2025sparsevdit}, and our local branch is neighbourhood attention~\citep{hassani2022nat}. Language models group distant positions, assign per-head spans, or separate retrieval heads from local ones~\citep{jin2024selfextend,fu2024moa,xiao2024duoattention}, and HiLo~\citep{pan2022hilo} and Shunted attention~\citep{ren2021shunted} train vision transformers whose heads split fine and coarse keys. We add measurements of how such head-specific mixtures transfer across scale.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}
\paragraph{Limitations.} \sca{} does not win everywhere. At $2048\times4096$ it has no resolved ImageReward advantage over AReA and loses on several other metrics, and UltraImage's more expensive two-stage recipe keeps higher preference scores. Small extra figures can still appear late in sampling, the exploratory $8192^2$ gallery retains grid artifacts, and one development prompt keeps doubled limbs in every variant (Appendix~\ref{app:failures}). The opening phase was chosen from development generations, not by the output-error objective. Transfer across emulated scales supports, but cannot prove, the hypothesis that the weights depend only on the image-to-window ratio. The full 191-prompt comparison uses FLUX.1-dev and one seed per prompt; extra seeds cover 46 prompts, and the FLUX.2-dev comparison covers 50 prompts against full attention only. We have not yet completed direct HRDiT, SigMa, or ResDiT baselines, and our guided UltraImage configuration does not cover its direct-extrapolation variant, so superiority over those settings is unestablished. Low-sample FID estimates and automatic preference models cannot replace human evaluation, and the intervals are not adjusted for multiple comparisons. \todo{E2, E3, E9: the $6144^2$ comparison, remaining large-resolution latency measurements, and Krea results remain pending.}

\paragraph{Conclusion.} How much each head relies on a neighbourhood fixed in tokens versus a view of the whole image can be measured once at the training resolution and reused at 4K. With that table and a global-only opening, training-free \sca{} beats AReA's released code at $4096^2$ and runs in about a third of its time.

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{lemma}[Softmax split]
\label{lem:split}
Let the keys of a query be partitioned into a near set $\Kset^{\mathrm{near}}$ and a far set $\Kset^{\mathrm{far}}$, with logits $\ell_j$. Then $\Ofull=m\,O^{\mathrm{near}}+(1-m)\,O^{\mathrm{far}}$ with $m=\sum_{j\in\Kset^{\mathrm{near}}}e^{\ell_j}/\sum_{j}e^{\ell_j}$ and $O^{\mathrm{near}},O^{\mathrm{far}}$ the softmax outputs restricted to $\Kset^{\mathrm{near}}$ and $\Kset^{\mathrm{far}}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $Z^{\mathrm{near}}=\sum_{j\in\Kset^{\mathrm{near}}}e^{\ell_j}$, so that $Z^{\mathrm{near}}O^{\mathrm{near}}=\sum_{j\in\Kset^{\mathrm{near}}}e^{\ell_j}v_j$, and likewise for the far set. Then $\Ofull=(Z^{\mathrm{near}}O^{\mathrm{near}}+Z^{\mathrm{far}}O^{\mathrm{far}})/(Z^{\mathrm{near}}+Z^{\mathrm{far}})$, and $Z^{\mathrm{near}}/(Z^{\mathrm{near}}+Z^{\mathrm{far}})=m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:range}]
\emph{Global operator.} Image queries sit at $c(p_i)\in\Gnat=[n_0]^2$ and text queries at $0\in\Gnat$; subsampled keys sit at $b\in\Gnat$ and text keys at $0$. Any two points of $[n_0]^2$ differ by at most $n_0-1$ per axis. The key count is $T+|\Gnat|=T+n_0^2$.

\emph{Local operator.} Fix one axis of length $H\ge w$ and a tile $[t\tau,\,t\tau+t)$ with $t\le w$. The unclamped start $a'=t\tau+\lfloor t/2\rfloor-\lfloor w/2\rfloor$ satisfies $a'\le t\tau$ because $\lfloor t/2\rfloor\le\lfloor w/2\rfloor$, and $a'+w\ge t\tau+t$ because $w-\lfloor w/2\rfloor=\lceil w/2\rceil\ge\lceil t/2\rceil=t-\lfloor t/2\rfloor$. If $a'<0$ the window is $[0,w)$, and $w\ge a'+w\ge t\tau+t$. If $a'>H-w$ the window is $[H-w,H)$, with $H-w<a'\le t\tau$ and $H\ge t\tau+t$. In every case the window contains the tile, so it contains each query of the tile, and two positions in a window of side $w\le n_0$ differ by at most $n_0-1$. The key count is $T+w^2=T+n_0^2$ when $n_0\le\min(H,W)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Text-image offsets]
\label{rem:text}
FLUX.1 places all text tokens at position 0, so the offset between an image query at $p_i$ and a text key is $p_i$. In the global operator the query sits at $c(p_i)\in[n_0]^2$, inside the trained range. In the local operator it keeps $p_i$, up to $sn_0-1$ per axis, exactly as in full attention and AReA's windows at the target. Mapping text offsets through $c(\cdot)$ in the local operator is a possible refinement that we have not tested.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:cost}]
Full attention scores every query against every key, $(N+T)^2$ pairs. The global operator scores $N+T$ queries against $T+|\Gnat|$ keys with $|\Gnat|=N/(s_Hs_W)=n_0^2$. The local operator scores $N$ image queries against $T$ text keys and $w^2$ window keys. The sum is at most $(2N+T)(T+w^2+N_0)$, which is $O(N(T+w^2+N_0))$ for fixed $n_0,T$. Value aggregation has the same pair counts. In the opening phase the local term is absent. The FlexAttention kernel visits whole blocks and masks the extra pairs: a query block of $8\times16$ tokens spans two tiles whose windows are 8 columns apart, so it visits a $64\times72$ key region, 12.5\% more than one window, plus partial blocks at clamped borders. This changes the constant, not the order.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:opt}]
$J(\pi)=E-2\pi B+\pi^2A$ with $A\ge0$. If $A=0$ then $\Oloc=\Oglob$ on every summand, so $B=0$ and $J$ is constant; any $\pi$, in particular $\pi^\star=1$, is optimal and the bound holds trivially. If $A>0$, $J$ is a strictly convex quadratic with unconstrained minimizer $B/A$, and its minimizer on $[0,1]$ is the projection $\clip(B/A,0,1)$. For any $\pi'$,
\[
J(\pi')-J(\pi^\star)=A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2+2(\pi'-\pi^\star)(A\pi^\star-B).
\]
If $0<\pi^\star<1$ then $A\pi^\star=B$ and the second term vanishes. If $\pi^\star=0$ then $B\le0$, so $A\pi^\star-B\ge0$ and $\pi'-\pi^\star\ge0$. If $\pi^\star=1$ then $B\ge A$, so $A\pi^\star-B\le0$ and $\pi'-\pi^\star\le0$. In both clipped cases the second term is nonnegative.

Any rule that assigns one weight per cell, such as a per-layer table, a weight shared per noise level, a hard switch, or a table fitted elsewhere, is a feasible $\pi'$ in every cell, so its total error is at least the per-head table's, by $\sum_{\text{cells}}A(\pi'-\pi^\star)^2$ or more. A table interpolated in $\sigma$ between the mean sampled noise levels of occupied bins is not constant within a bin; the statement applies to the bin-constant table used to score rules in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The frequency argument in more detail.} Argument~\ref{arg:freq} rests on two facts and one assumption. The first fact is that the noisy latent is a sum of signal and independent white noise, so a band's signal-to-noise ratio scales as $((1-\sigma)/\sigma)^2S(f)$; at $\sigma=0.89$ the factor is $0.015$, and at $\sigma=0.5$ it is 1. The second is that a window of $w$ tokens cannot distinguish a component of period longer than $w$ from a smooth trend within the window, so windows cannot coordinate a layout at that scale except through what earlier layers mixed into their keys. The assumption is that natural-image latent spectra fall with frequency, which we have not measured for FLUX.1 latents. Together they predict that windowed attention fails first and worst at high noise and for objects larger than the window, which matches the duplication we observe (Figure~\ref{fig:failure}) and the effect of a 16-token window.

\paragraph{Statistics.} For every emulated scale, block, head, and noise bin, the pass stores $A$, $B$, $E$, the error of the local branch alone $\sum\|\Ofull-\Oloc\|^2$, $\sum\|\Ofull\|^2$, and the query count, in float64, summed over image queries and channels (\texttt{sca/calib.py}). The sampler's trajectory is that of unmodified full attention; the emulated operators only observe. Statistics are written per prompt and merged by summation, so prompts can be added later and split-half fits need no extra pass. Text-query outputs are not in the objective, because \sca{} sends text queries through the global branch only.

\paragraph{Geometry at emulated scale.} At $1024^2$ the grid is $64\times64$. Emulated scale 2 uses a $32\times32$ window, $2\times2$ subsampling onto a $32\times32$ grid, and $4\times4$ query tiles; emulated scale 4 uses a $16\times16$ window, $4\times4$ subsampling onto a $16\times16$ grid, and $2\times2$ tiles. At $1280^2$ the grid is $80\times80$, and emulated scale 2 uses a $40\times40$ window, $2\times2$ subsampling onto a $40\times40$ grid, and $5\times5$ tiles.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{\sca{}: calibration (once per model) and one attention layer at sampling time.}
  \label{alg:sca}
  \small
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Statex \textbf{Calibrate}(prompts, $s_c$) on an in-distribution $n\times n$ grid:
    \State sample with full attention; at every block $l$, step $\sigma$, and head $h$:
    \State \quad $\Oglob,\Oloc\gets$ Eqs.~\ref{eq:glob}, \ref{eq:loc} with subsampling $s_c$, window $n/s_c$, tile $tn/(s_cn_0)$
    \State \quad add $\|\Oloc{-}\Oglob\|^2$ and $\langle\Ofull{-}\Oglob,\Oloc{-}\Oglob\rangle$ to $A_{lh\beta(\sigma)}$, $B_{lh\beta(\sigma)}$ \Comment{float64}
    \State \Return $\pi_{lh\beta}\gets\clip(B_{lh\beta}/A_{lh\beta},0,1)$ \Comment{Proposition~\ref{prop:opt}}
    \Statex \textbf{Layer}$(Q,K,V,\sigma)$ at target factors $(s_H,s_W)$:
    \State $\Oglob\gets$ Eq.~\ref{eq:glob} for all queries \Comment{block-centre keys, coarse positions}
    \If{$\sigma>\sigma_g$} \Return $\Oglob$ \Comment{opening phase}
    \EndIf
    \State $\Oloc\gets$ Eq.~\ref{eq:loc} for image queries \Comment{one FlexAttention call}
    \State \Return $[\,\Oglob_{\Tset},\;\pih\,\Oloc+(1-\pih)\,\Oglob_{\Gset}\,]$ \Comment{interpolated table; fp32 mix}
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{FlexAttention window.} Image tokens are reordered tile-major, so that a sparse block is a small spatial tile. Query blocks cover $8\times16$ tokens (128 queries) and key blocks $8\times8$ (64 keys), shifted so that unclamped windows start on a block edge; text keys come first. The mask function states window membership per query-key pair, so the result does not depend on the block layout, only the amount of skipped work does. On GH200, $8\times16$ query blocks with $8\times8$ key blocks were fastest at $2048^2$, $4096^2$, and $2048\times6144$. RoPE, subsampling, both branches, and the mix compile into one graph per layer; the mix is in fp32.

\paragraph{Numerical checks.} The 18 CPU tests in \texttt{tests/test\_sca.py} use float32 to check full-mode agreement with stock FLUX attention, both branches against brute-force Equations~\ref{eq:glob} and~\ref{eq:loc}, full-grid and factor-1 identities, calibration when the local branch is exact, the gate, and FlexAttention against an explicit gather reference. A separate GPU check in \texttt{scripts/bench\_attn.py} compares the gather and FlexAttention implementations in bf16: the recorded maximum absolute difference is 0.015625, and both have the same maximum error against the fp32 reference on the tested grids. This is an observed absolute-error check; no per-element ULP bound was measured.

\paragraph{Position ids in bf16.} The FLUX.1 pipeline builds image position ids in bf16, which cannot represent every integer above 256. Our frozen runs retain these rounded ids for RoPE. Passing the true grid from the sampler fixes window membership and subsampling geometry, but does not restore exact RoPE coordinates: the coarse-query positions are also computed from the rounded ids. At a 384-token axis, index 383 rounds to 384 and its scale-6 coarse coordinate becomes 64; at a 512-token axis, index 511 rounds to 512 and its scale-8 coarse coordinate also becomes 64. A local 64-token window can likewise have a rounded endpoint difference of 64 instead of the ideal bound 63. Proposition~\ref{prop:range} therefore applies exactly to the mathematical operator, not to these finite-precision positions at $2048\times6144$, $6144^2$, or $8192^2$. The $4096^2$ grid uses indices 0--255, which bf16 represents exactly. FLUX.2 uses integer position ids. Rebuilding FLUX.1 positions in higher precision would require a separate controlled experiment; the reported runs are unchanged.

\paragraph{Non-divisible grids.} When $H/n_0$ is not an integer, the subsampling factor per axis is the divisor of $H$ nearest to $H/n_0$, and the tile side the divisor nearest to $t$; neither case arises at the sizes we evaluate.

\paragraph{Prompts and references.} The Aesthetic-4K evaluation split~\citep{zhang2025diffusion4k} has 195 prompts with 4K reference images. Four (boats, Moai, woman and bear, underwater surfer) were used to choose the opening phase and window and are excluded from generation evaluation, leaving 191. Distribution metrics retain all 195 official reference rows. The 46-prompt ablation subset was fixed before any ablation was run. HRGen-1K~\citep{rajabi2026area} has 1000 prompts and no references; we use source rows 0--199, with ids \texttt{hrgen\_0000}--\texttt{hrgen\_0199}. The 48 calibration prompts were written for this purpose and share no prompt with Aesthetic-4K (the largest word-set Jaccard overlap with any evaluation prompt is 0.26).

\paragraph{Preference and alignment metrics.} ImageReward v1.0~\citep{xu2023imagereward} (bicubic resize to 224, centre crop). HPSv2 and HPSv2.1~\citep{wu2023hpsv2}: cosine of normalized image and text features with the official validation transform (longest side 224, padded). PickScore~\citep{kirstain2023pickapic}: $\exp(\text{logit scale})$ times the cosine. CLIP score~\citep{radford2021clip}: $100\max(\cos,0)$ with ViT-L/14. Aesthetic: the LAION improved aesthetic predictor (sac+logos+ava1, linear MSE) on the L2-normalized CLIP ViT-L/14 image embedding~\citep{schuhmann2022aesthetic}.

\paragraph{No-reference quality.} MUSIQ~\citep{ke2021musiq} (pyiqa defaults) on the full image at native resolution, and MUSIQ$_\text{c}$, its mean over four fixed random $1024^2$ crops per image; crop positions depend only on the prompt id, so they are identical across methods. CLIP-IQA~\citep{wang2022clipiqa} and ARNIQA~\citep{agnolucci2024arniqa} (pyiqa defaults) on the image downscaled with antialiased bicubic resampling to a long side of 1024 pixels, the scale both were trained on.

\paragraph{Distribution metrics and reference correction.} FID~\citep{heusel2017fid} and KID compare 191 generated images with all 195 official reference rows, using Inception-v3 features (torch-fidelity) after bilinear resizing to $299^2$. The reference rows contain 168 distinct image contents; repeated rows retain their official weights. Patch metrics use four $1024^2$ crops per row (764 generated and 780 reference crops). An audit found that the original feature-bank assembly keyed reference crops by resolved image path, so repeated images reused the last row's crop features. We corrected the lookup to retain each row's intended cached crops. Whole-image FID/KID are unchanged; all patch point estimates reported here use the corrected assembly. Original CSVs remain archived for provenance. Inception score uses full images at $299^2$, with 10 splits.

\paragraph{Intervals.} Per-image intervals are 95\% paired percentile bootstraps over prompt ids: original summaries use 1000 draws; separately recomputed comparisons use 2000 draws with seed 0. For the main \sca{}--AReA FID/KID and patch-FID/KID comparison, 1000 draws resample generated prompt ids jointly across methods and resample the 168 distinct reference-image identities, keeping all rows and crops from each sampled identity together. This retains the 195-row weighted point estimate while accounting for repeated reference images. The resulting FID difference is $-7.63$ $[-12.79,-2.94]$; patch FID is $-3.60$ $[-7.39,1.15]$ and patch KID ($\times10^3$) is $-2.27$ $[-4.73,0.12]$. An earlier version of this analysis reported a significant patch-KID improvement; under the corrected evaluation its interval includes zero. The whole-image and patch FID/KID comparisons at both non-square sizes, the added baselines' patch comparisons, and the 56-step patch-FID comparison also use reference-image clusters. Other whole-image FID/KID intervals are labelled historical reference-row bootstraps; they have not been recomputed with image-identity clusters. Historical patch intervals are not attached to corrected estimates and are omitted where recomputation is incomplete. FID's finite-sample bias can place point estimates outside percentile intervals. Intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons and measure prompt/reference sampling uncertainty, not variability over independent model training runs.

\section{Reproducing AReA}
\label{app:area}

We run AReA's released code with its FLUX.1 defaults (block-centre pooling, first phase for 8 of 28 steps, halo width 8, bridge ratio 0.5), the FLUX.1-dev checkpoint, guidance 4.5, 28 steps, $\mu$ at its maximum, and the seeds shared with the other methods. AReA's paper reports ImageReward 1.32 at 4K under its own settings; our shared protocol gives 0.92, so cross-paper numbers are not comparable, while our direct \sca{}--AReA comparison holds these settings fixed. UltraImage and FLUX.2-dev use separate protocols (Appendix~\ref{app:additional_protocols}). AReA's paper reports 151\,s for AReA and 314\,s for full attention at $4096^2$ on an H100; the matched sweep measures 202.4\,s and 186.5\,s on one GH200 (medians of three images). We use commit \texttt{9e38ec2} of the released repository, which includes a cost cap on halo and bridge tokens and a jagged halo edge; neither appears in AReA's paper, which does not say whether its Table~1 used them. Recomputed from the code with 512 text tokens, the first-phase pooling factors are 4, 4, 4, 4, 2, 2, 2, 2 at $4096\times4096$ and $2048\times4096$, and 8, 8, 8, 4, 4, 4, 2, 2 at $2048\times6144$. The second-phase windows shrink over 20 steps from 180, 151, and 192 tokens respectively to 64. At $2048\times6144$ no window is cheaper than full attention once the text tokens are counted, so the first window falls back to 192 tokens and the cap drops the bridge and halo on the first three second-phase steps. Other differences from the release defaults are the FLUX.1-dev checkpoint instead of FLUX.1-Krea-dev, and one seed per prompt shared across methods instead of one seed for all prompts. \todo{E9: AReA with its default FLUX.1-Krea-dev checkpoint, against recalibrated \sca{}.}

\section{Other baselines and FLUX.2-dev}
\label{app:additional_protocols}

\paragraph{DyPE and SEGA.} We use the existing baseline harness frozen at commit \texttt{c05ebdc}, with FLUX.1-dev, 28 steps, guidance 4.5, maximum shift, tiled VAE decoding, and the same prompt-derived seeds as \sca{}. These settings isolate the attention/position treatment on the checkpoint used in our main comparison. They do not reproduce the methods' reported FLUX.1-Krea-dev results; the separate Krea comparison remains pending. Their low FLUX.1-dev scores should therefore not be read as a ranking under each method's preferred checkpoint.

\paragraph{UltraImage.} We run a guided two-stage configuration from the FLUX branch of the released implementation through \texttt{scripts/run\_ultraimage.py}. It uses a $1024^2$ first stage and a $4096^2$ second stage, 30 steps per stage, guidance 3.5 then 7, the \texttt{super\_imax} guidance schedule, shifted-window patchification, proportional attention, RIFLEx positions, and attention decay. Each prompt also needs a 2+2-step block-average preparation pass. The reported campaign time includes preparation when not cached and the two-stage call; main-stage and preparation times are retained separately in sidecars. Each call reseeds both the generator and the global RNG, including the VAE sampling path, from the prompt id and seed label. Thus seed labels are paired, but the two-stage method does not share an identical latent trajectory with the one-stage methods. The wrapper preserves an upstream scheduler behaviour: assigning \texttt{scheduler.config.shift} does not update the scheduler's internal shift in the installed diffusers version, so both stages retain shift 3.0. This is a reproduction caveat, not a newly tuned setting. We report this system-level comparison separately from the common-sampler attention comparisons.

\paragraph{FLUX.2-dev.} The model has 56 attention blocks and 48 heads per block. We reuse the two branches and calibration rule with a native grid of $64^2$ tokens, local window 64, and query tile 8. The four-axis positions preserve the time and text axes while coarsening the two spatial axes. Calibration uses 16 prompts at $1024^2$, 14 noise bins, and emulated scales 2 and 4; sampling uses the scale-4 table. Both full attention and \sca{} use 28 steps, guidance 4.0, seed label 0, and a shift fixed to the native sequence length, $\mu=2.1514$. The pipeline's default extrapolated shift at $4096^2$ would be about 11.55; we therefore do not interpret this comparison as a default-pipeline benchmark. The gate is 0.959 so that the first 8 steps use only global attention. The 50 evaluation prompts are the first 50 Aesthetic-4K prompts remaining after development-prompt exclusion. Prompt embeddings are cached for both methods; reported times include denoising and tiled VAE decoding, and exclude text encoding and model loading. At emulated scale 4, fitted per-head relative output error is 0.1790, versus 0.1889 for per-layer weights and 0.1924 for local-only attention; split-half correlation is 0.950 and cross-half error 0.1794. These are calibration diagnostics, not generation ablations of the individual components on FLUX.2.

\section{Sampling-budget and guidance checks}
\label{app:budget}

On the same 46 evaluation prompts, we compare the default 28-step \sca{} run with 56 steps at guidance 4.5 and with 28 steps at guidance 7 (Table~\ref{tab:phase2_budget}). The 56-step run keeps the noise threshold at 0.89; it therefore changes the discretization of both the opening and mixed phases. It improves ImageReward by 0.097 $[0.039,0.159]$, HPSv2.1 by 1.01, PickScore by 0.16, and MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ by 2.26, but increases corrected patch FID by 7.05 $[1.16,13.03]$ with reference-image cluster resampling. Increasing guidance to 7 alone lowers HPSv2.1 and PickScore and does not resolve an ImageReward difference. Thus the default schedule does not maximize every metric, and extra sampling can change the preference-quality balance.

Against UltraImage on this subset, 56-step \sca{} has ImageReward difference $-0.038$ $[-0.159,0.078]$, HPSv2.1 difference $-0.56$ $[-1.21,0.09]$, and PickScore difference $-0.19$ $[-0.40,0.01]$. These intervals span zero and do not establish equivalence. Its MUSIQ advantage is 9.94 $[5.69,13.98]$ and its MUSIQ$_\text{c}$ advantage 21.83 $[18.67,24.73]$. Campaign means are 71.6\,s for default \sca{}, 140.1\,s for 56-step \sca{}, and 322.0\,s for UltraImage on these 46 prompts. This is a sampling-budget sensitivity check, not an equal-FLOP or equal-wall-clock experiment: UltraImage has two resolutions, different guidance, and per-prompt preparation.

\section{The low-band duplication guard}
\label{app:band}

\paragraph{Construction.} Let $\LP$ average-pool a head's image output over $p\times p$ tokens and upsample it bilinearly, and $\HP=\mathrm{I}-\LP$. The guard replaces the low band of the mix $M$ by that of the global branch:
\begin{equation}
  O \;=\; \LP\big(\Oglob\big) + \HP\big(M\big) \;=\; \Oglob + \pih\,\HP\big(\Oloc-\Oglob\big).
\end{equation}
The local branch then contributes only its difference from the global branch above the cutoff. The cutoff should lie below $1/(2s)$, where the global branch aliases, and above $1/w$, below which the window cannot resolve; with $w=64$ and $s=4$ this leaves periods of 8 to 64 tokens. We used $p=16$; $p=8$ and $p=32$ were worse on four development prompts, and a noise-dependent cutoff behaved like $p=16$. FreeScale~\citep{qiu2024freescale} also fuses two self-attention outputs by frequency band, in the opposite direction: it keeps the blurred low band of attention over overlapping crops and takes the high band from full self-attention at the target size. ResDiT also combines global low frequencies and local high frequencies, using patch-wise FFT masks~\citep{ma2025resdit}. Our optional guard uses pooled global attention and preserves the fitted per-head weight on the local-minus-global high-band residual. Its global-low/local-high construction is therefore not itself novel.

\paragraph{History: what it fixes and what it costs.} On the development prompts, the band alone (no opening phase) fixed the boats but left extra statues and faces on the Moai and woman-and-bear prompts; the opening phase alone got the main subject right, but small extra figures appeared late on some prompts; both together gave one subject on the boats, Moai, and woman-and-bear prompts. This was our first full configuration. On 46 prompts, however, removing the guard improves HPSv2 ($+0.54$), HPSv2.1 ($+1.17$), PickScore ($+0.25$), CLIP-IQA ($+1.7$), and MUSIQ ($+2.9$), all significant, leaves the other metrics within noise, and saves 17\,s per image on an idle GPU. The guard also hid what the per-head mix contributes. With it (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_band}), local only matched the per-head mix within 2\% on preference metrics, because the guard replaces the low band of every head's output with that of the global branch whatever the head's weight, so it injects global low frequencies even at $\pi=1$; without the guard local only loses on nearly every metric (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}). Likewise, with the guard one shared weight and per-layer weights matched the per-head table on preference metrics and lost only on no-reference quality, and hard per-head routing (10 to 17\% of heads global at threshold 0.5, about 6\% at 0.3) stayed within 2\% of the mix. The guard is an option for prompts where late duplication is visible, not part of the default method.

\section{Compute and reproducibility}
\label{app:compute}

\paragraph{Compute.} Generation and calibration used single NVIDIA GH200 GPUs on a shared cluster; the verified timing logs identify the GH200 120\,GB model. Some metric evaluations ran on CPUs. The surviving logs do not establish the complete calibration runtime. Generation at $4096^2$ takes about 71\,s per image for \sca{}, 262\,s for AReA, and 197\,s for full attention on shared nodes (campaign means); the main comparisons, ablations, and non-square sizes comprise more than 2000 generated images. Surviving worker logs establish a lower bound of 123.3 GH200 worker-hours through 2026-10-17 06:59 UTC, including model loading, calibration, evaluation, and failed attempts. The bound is the union of 581 closed task intervals per physical node, with timestamp precision treated conservatively; it measures occupied worker time, not GPU kernel activity. Missing historical logs, unmatched starts, and ongoing work prevent a complete campaign total. Overlapping scheduler allocation time and image-sidecar durations are not added to this bound.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} The code (attention processor, calibration, sampling, evaluation wrappers, AReA wrapper, and tests), the calibration table \texttt{k1\_1024.npz}, the per-image metric files, and the per-comparison summary and paired-delta CSV files behind every table will be released. Main-text metric values and paired intervals are derived from those CSV files. Each \sca{} generation task ran from a frozen copy of the code named in the task. Per-image sidecar files record the sampling settings and, where available, a code fingerprint; the inherited Krea baseline records do not establish one source revision for every image.

\FloatBarrier
\section{Broader impact}
\label{app:impact}

\sca{} shortens generation at $4096^2$ relative to full attention under our measured protocol, which helps applications such as print, design, and scientific visualization. Energy use remains unmeasured. The same reduction lowers the cost of producing high-resolution synthetic images that could be used to mislead. \sca{} adds no capability beyond the base model's and does not change its safety filters or watermarking; the risks and mitigations of the base model apply unchanged.

\section{Failure cases}
\label{app:failures}

\snippet{failures}{}

Figure~\ref{fig:failures} shows the duplication that windows cause without the gate, and local errors that \sca{} still makes.

\paragraph{Late duplication.} With the final method, small extra figures can appear on some prompts in the last 20 steps, when the local branch dominates most heads. The band guard suppresses some of them but costs preference scores. \paragraph{Articulated subjects.} The underwater-surfer development prompt keeps doubled limbs in every variant; AReA's image shows a large translucent ghost. \paragraph{Small factors.} At $2048\times4096$ the window covers half the image height, so the local branch alone already sees most of the image along that axis, and the per-head mix gains little over AReA's shrinking windows; aesthetic score and corrected patch FID are significantly worse. \paragraph{Larger scales and another backbone.} The gallery sampled from the completed 12-prompt $8192^2$ run still shows grid and stripe artifacts in both methods (Figure~\ref{fig:gallery_8192_complete}); this exploratory set does not establish general 8K quality. In FLUX.2-dev, improved aggregate metrics coexist with distorted facial geometry and fine texture in the randomly selected portrait example (Figure~\ref{fig:gallery_flux2}). \paragraph{Text-image offsets.} The local branch keeps out-of-range text-image offsets (Remark~\ref{rem:text}). \paragraph{Metrics.} No-reference quality metrics reward sharp repeated subjects (v0 scores higher on CLIP-IQA and ARNIQA), and FID against 195 references at $299^2$ resolution sees little of the 4K detail; we therefore report every metric and the qualitative galleries.
