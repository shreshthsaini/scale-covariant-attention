Scale-Covariant AttentionPer-Head Receptive Fields Calibrated at the Training Resolution

Training-free 4096² sampling for diffusion transformers. Each head's reach is measured once at 1024², where full attention still works.

Abstract

Diffusion transformers trained near one megapixel fail at 4096²: attention meets token counts and position offsets it never saw, at 256 times the cost. Training-free fixes set how far each head looks by hand or per target size, because at the target full attention fails and cannot serve as a reference. At the training resolution, where full attention is still valid, reach is a property of the head. After the first steps, the head explains 97.5% of the variance in how much attention stays near the query, and the noise level 2.5%. We introduce Scale-Covariant Attention (SCA), which gives each head its own mix of a native-size window and a native-size grid over the whole image. Shrinking both views at 1024² to the target's image-to-window ratio lets the model's own full attention fit each mix in closed form, once for every size. Inside HRDiT's three-stage schedule, SCA on FLUX.1-dev at 4096² beats AReA on all ten per-image metrics for each of three seeds at 4.1× its speed, and matches HRDiT's preference scores in 49.2 against 107.0 s per image. A single-pass mode keeps more fine detail and runs in 77.1 s, 2.63× faster than AReA.

Reach is a property of the head

A fix that restricts attention must decide how far each head looks. At 4096² nothing can check that decision, because full attention there is the failure. At 1024², where full attention is still correct, the decision can be measured.

For random queries in every block and step, we recorded the share of each head's image attention within 16 tokens of the query. After the first 8 steps, the variance of that share over all heads and noise levels splits as follows.

97.5%
is explained by the head: 16.6% between blocks, 80.9% between heads of one block
2.5%
by the noise level within a head
0.7%
by the head-averaged curve, the part one schedule for all heads can follow. It rises from 0.57 to 0.62 as denoising proceeds, the narrowing AReA reports.
97.6% and 99.1%
is the head's part on FLUX.1-Krea-dev and FLUX.2-dev, and at least 96.9% for every model, size and radius in the paper

SCA therefore calibrates every head on its own and reuses the result at every size through the image-to-window ratio.

Method

SCA replaces only the attention, so it runs inside any sampler. Each head mixes two views that keep the training geometry: an exact native-size window around the query, and a native-size grid of block-centre keys over the whole image. We fit the mix by emulation at 1024². Shrinking both views to the target's image-to-window ratio lets the model's own full attention score them, and the best weight has a closed form. One calibration pass serves every target size.

Each component up close

The appendix version of the diagram. (a) The global operator on block centres. (b) The local operator with its tiles, windows, bridge and block mask. (c) The gate and the per-head mix. (d) The calibrated table at r = 4 for all 57 blocks.

1Two views per head

The local view is an exact sliding window around each 8 × 8 query tile, at the original positions, plus the text and 1,024 bridge tokens from the whole image. The global view attends to the centre token of every block on a 64 × 64 grid, with coordinates rescaled to that grid. The cost grows linearly with the number of tokens.

2One table for every size

To emulate an image r times larger, we shrink the window and subsample the global view by r at 1024². The best weight per head, noise level and ratio has a closed form. One pass over 32 prompts fills the ratio-2 and ratio-4 tables for all 1368 heads; tables from disjoint prompt halves correlate at 0.989.

3Progressive SCA, the headline

HRDiT's released schedule, kept as is: 30 steps at 1024² with the model's own attention, then decode, upsample, sharpen, re-encode and re-noise for the last 17 steps at 2048² and the last 10 at 4096². SCA replaces HRDiT's attention changes at both upper stages, reading ratio 2 and then 4 from one table.

4Single-pass SCA, the fast mode

All 28 steps run at the target. The first 8 use the global view alone, as AReA does; windows of 128, 112, 96, 80 tokens then hand over to the native 64 for the last 16. The opening, the window sizes and the bridge are set by hand; only the weights are calibrated.

Progressive SCA, HRDiT's schedule. 30 steps at 1024² with full attention. The 2048² stage re-enters at σ = 0.81 for the last 17 steps, the 4096² stage at σ = 0.61 for the last 10; guidance is 3.5, 4.5 and 6.0. Both upper stages start below the gate and the last wide window, so they run the native window, the bridge and the calibrated mix. The 1024² stage takes the place of the global opening.

Single-pass SCA. The 28 steps at 4096²: global view only, then windows of 128, 112, 96, 80 tokens at ratios 2, 2.29, 2.67 and 3.2, then the native window at ratio 4. Every local softmax after the opening also holds the bridge. In each cell of an 8 × 8 partition it takes the 16 tokens whose keys lie farthest from the mean key, recomputed at every block and step with AReA's selection rule. Each window thus also sees tokens from the whole image.

Why the opening is set by hand. Output error cannot see layout. Fitted on output error alone, the best hard switch is local attention from the first step, and the per-head mix without an opening loses 1.33 ImageReward at 4096². On the 46-prompt ablation subset, against SCA without the schedule, that difference is −1.33 [−1.60, −1.06]. The global opening, or the 1024² stage of progressive SCA, therefore sets the layout.

Progressive SCA against HRDiT: same schedule, different attention

  • Preference scores: no resolved difference. ImageReward −0.02 [−0.09, 0.03], HPSv2.1 +0.10 [−0.14, 0.35] (×100) and PickScore −0.05 [−0.14, 0.04]. Intervals that include zero do not establish equivalence.
  • Quality scores split. Progressive SCA is better on HPSv2, MUSIQ, ARNIQA, with MUSIQ +1.3 [0.2, 2.4], and worse on CLIP-IQA by −0.9 [−1.8, −0.1] (×100).
  • Time. 49.2 s against 107.0 s per image, timed back to back on one GPU (2.2×); campaign means 50.7 s against 110.4 s.
  • Set level. FID is unresolved (+3.4 [−1.8, 7.2]), and HRDiT has lower KID, patch FID (+4.6 [0.6, 7.7] for progressive SCA) and patch KID.

Progressive against single-pass SCA: preference for fine detail

  • Progressive SCA gains preference. ImageReward +0.07 [0.01, 0.13], PickScore +0.29 [0.20, 0.38] and HPSv2.1 +1.29 [0.99, 1.59] (×100).
  • Single-pass SCA keeps more fine detail. Progressive SCA loses MUSIQ (−3.4 [−5.1, −1.6]) and MUSIQ on 1024² crops (−6.0 [−7.3, −4.6]), and its patch FID is higher by +9.2 [5.1, 13.2]. FID is unresolved (+1.6 [−2.3, 7.3]).
  • Steps at 4096². Progressive SCA runs 10 steps there, on an upsampled image; single-pass SCA runs all 28 at the target.
  • Time. Campaign means of 50.7 s for progressive and 78.4 s for single-pass SCA on shared nodes.

Progressive SCA against the other baselines

  • AReA, seed 0. Better on 10 of 10 per-image metrics: ImageReward +0.40 [0.32, 0.49], PickScore +1.05 [0.93, 1.19], HPSv2.1 +3.86 [3.41, 4.30] (×100).
  • AReA, set level, seed 0. FID is unresolved (−3.9 [−8.5, 3.1]). Patch FID (+4.8 [0.1, 10.6]) and patch KID are worse for progressive SCA.
  • DyPE, SEGA, full attention. Better on 10, 10 and 10 of 10 per-image metrics. All three are also worse than progressive SCA on FID and patch FID.
  • UltraImage. Better on 9 of 10 per-image metrics; aesthetic score is unresolved. On set-level scores, FID (−0.2 [−3.8, 2.5]) and patch FID (−1.9 [−3.7, 0.5]) are unresolved.

More seeds, prompts, sizes and checkpoints

  • Progressive SCA, three seeds. Progressive SCA beats AReA on all ten per-image metrics at each of the three seed labels. ImageReward rises by +0.40 [0.32, 0.49], +0.37 [0.29, 0.46] and +0.35 [0.27, 0.45] at seeds 0, 1 and 2, PickScore by +1.05, +1.00 and +0.99.
  • Patch FID across seeds. The patch FID gap to AReA is significant at seeds 0 and 2 (+4.8 [0.1, 10.6] and +5.4 [0.8, 10.8]) but not at seed 1 (+1.5 [−2.7, 6.9]). Progressive SCA on HRGen-1K: pending.
  • Single pass, two more seeds. With seed labels 1 and 2 on all 195 prompts, single-pass SCA beats AReA on 10 and 10 of the ten per-image metrics; ImageReward +0.306 and +0.264.
  • Single pass, HRGen-1K. On all 1000 prompts, single-pass SCA beats AReA on 10 of 10 per-image metrics; ImageReward +0.764 [0.714, 0.816].
  • FLUX.1-Krea-dev, single pass. The progressive sampler does not run this checkpoint. Single-pass SCA wins 7 of 10 per-image metrics against AReA and loses 0, at 3.1× its speed (78.4 s against 239.3 s). ImageReward is unresolved (+0.020 [−0.020, 0.063]) and patch FID is worse (+3.4 [1.6, 7.1]).
  • Panoramas, single pass. At 2048 × 6144, single-pass SCA beats AReA on 7 of 15 metrics at 60.5 s against 150.5 s, but loses on aesthetic score, patch FID.
  • 2048 × 4096 is mixed. The window already covers half the image height, so the per-head mix adds little over AReA's windows. Single-pass SCA is better on 3 of 15 metrics and worse on 3 (aesthetic score, CLIP score, CLIP-IQA), at 41.1 s against 112.4 s.
  • Larger squares (previous operator). At 6144², SCA without the schedule beats AReA on 14 of 15 metrics; single-pass SCA takes 174.7 s per image there.
  • FLUX.2-dev (previous operator). Calibrated on its own, SCA without the schedule beats full attention on 9 of 10 per-image metrics at 2.0× its speed; ImageReward rises from 0.75 to 1.20 (50 prompts).

Against a commercial model

This section sets single-pass SCA on FLUX.1-dev, run on one GPU without training, beside Ideogram 4.0, a commercial generator, on six of the same prompts. Each pair uses the same prompt text and shows crops of 512 × 512 native pixels from each image, centred on the same point of the scene. Ideogram 4.0 generates up to about 4 megapixels (2048 × 2048 for a square) and reaches 4096² only through a separate upscaler; SCA samples 4096² (16.8 megapixels) directly. The six prompts come from our curated set, which favours SCA; the Ideogram images are first results, not picked.

The Ideogram images have not been added yet, so the right-hand panels are labelled placeholders. Ideogram's own published samples carry no permission to reuse them, so this page shows only images generated for it (instructions in web/README.md).

Limitations

  • Fidelity. Progressive SCA trades fine detail for preference against single-pass SCA (MUSIQ on 1024² crops −6.0, patch FID +9.2). Its patch FID and patch KID are worse than HRDiT's, and its patch FID is higher than AReA's at two of three seeds.
  • Hand-set settings. The opening, the window sizes and the bridge are set by hand, because output error cannot see layout. We chose the window sizes on the 46-prompt subset, which is part of the evaluation set.
  • Dependence on HRDiT. Progressive SCA depends on HRDiT's schedule, and its sampler does not support FLUX.1-Krea-dev.
  • Previous operator. The 6144², 8192², FLUX.2-dev and sensitivity results use SCA without the schedule.
  • Calibration. Calibration omits the bridge and sees 16 times fewer keys per branch than the target; the deployed operator's error is untested.
  • Metrics. All metrics are automatic, and the intervals ignore multiple comparisons. FID at 299² sees little of the 4K detail.
  • Weaker settings. At 2048 × 4096 single-pass SCA loses 3 metrics to AReA, and on FLUX.1-Krea-dev it loses patch FID.
  • 8192². Grid and stripe artifacts remain in both SCA and AReA; the 12 prompts there are exploratory and use the previous operator.
  • FLUX.2-dev. With the previous operator, better average metrics coexist with distorted faces in a randomly drawn portrait.
