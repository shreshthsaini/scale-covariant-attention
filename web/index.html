Abstract

Abstract

Diffusion transformers trained near one megapixel fail at 4096²: attention meets token counts and position offsets it never saw, at 256 times the cost. Training-free fixes set how far each head looks by hand or per target size, because at the target full attention fails and cannot serve as a reference. At the training resolution, where full attention is still valid, reach is a property of the head. After the first steps, the head explains 97.5% of the variance in how much attention stays near the query, and the noise level 2.5%. We introduce Scale-Covariant Attention (SCA), which gives each head its own mix of a native-size window and a native-size grid over the whole image. Reach emulation shrinks both views at 1024² to the target's image-to-window ratio, so that the model's own full attention fits each mix in closed form, once for every size. Inside HRDiT's three-stage schedule, SCA on FLUX.1-dev at 4096² beats AReA on all ten per-image metrics for each of three seeds and on HRGen-1K at 4.1× its speed, and matches HRDiT's preference scores in 49.2 against 107.0 s per image. A single-pass mode keeps more fine detail and runs in 77.1 s, 2.63× faster than AReA.

In motion

The idea in two minutes

The observation

Reach is a property of the head

A fix that restricts attention must decide how far each head looks. At 4096² nothing can check that decision, because full attention there is the failure. At 1024², where full attention still works, the decision can be measured.

For 256 queries in every block and step at 1024², we record the share of each head's image attention within 16 tokens of the query. After the first 8 steps, the variance of that share over all heads and noise levels splits three ways.

97.5%the head: 16.6% between blocks and 80.9% between heads of one block
2.5%the noise level, within a head
0.7%the head-averaged curve, the only part one schedule for all heads can follow

The averaged curve rises from 0.57 to 0.62 during sampling, the narrowing AReA reports. On FLUX.1-Krea-dev and FLUX.2-dev the head's part is 97.6% and 99.1%.

The method

Two views per head, one measured weight

SCA replaces only the attention, so it runs inside any sampler. Each head mixes two views that keep the training geometry. The weight of the mix is fit once at 1024² and read at every size through the image-to-window ratio.

1 · Near and far

Full attention is already a mix

Split one query's keys into a near set and a far set. Full attention is then exactly

O = m Onear + (1 − m) Ofar,

where m is the softmax mass of the near set and each O attends to its own set. Heads differ mostly in m. The three heads in the figure, measured at 1024², keep very different shares of their attention within 16 tokens.

2 · Two views

A native window and a native view of the whole image

Local: an exact 64 × 64-token window around each 8 × 8 query tile at its original positions, plus the text and 1,024 bridge tokens from the whole image. Global: one real token per block, a 64 × 64 grid over the whole image with its coordinates rescaled to that grid. Each head returns

Oh = πh Oloc + (1 − πh) Oglob.

Every offset in both views stays inside the range seen in training, bridge keys aside, so the model keeps its own RoPE. The cost grows linearly with the number of tokens.

3 · Reach emulation

Measured where full attention still works

At 4096² full attention is the failure, so it cannot judge πh. At 1024² it can. We fit the mix by reach emulation: shrink the window to n/r tokens and subsample the global grid by r. The image-to-window ratio is then r, as at a target r times larger, and the model's own full attention and both shrunken views see the same queries, keys and values.

Ratio 2 stands for 2048², ratio 4 for 4096². The weights depend mainly on the ratio: the 1024² reach table scored at 1280² is as good as one fitted there.

4 · Closed form

The best weight is one division

For each block, head, noise level and ratio, three sums over prompts, steps, queries and channels give the squared error of any weight:

J(π) = E − 2πB + π2A,    π* = clip(B / A, 0, 1),

with A = Σ‖Oloc − Oglob‖2, B = Σ⟨Ofull − Oglob, Oloc − Oglob⟩ and E = Σ‖Ofull − Oglob‖2. One emulation pass over 32 prompts fills the reach table for ratios 2 and 4. Reach tables fitted on disjoint halves of the prompts correlate at 0.989.

5 · Sampling

Progressive SCA, the headline

We keep HRDiT's released three-stage schedule as is: 30 steps at 1024² with the model's own attention set the layout. The image is then decoded, upsampled, sharpened, re-encoded and re-noised for the last 17 steps at 2048² and the last 10 at 4096². SCA replaces HRDiT's attention changes at both upper stages, reading ratio 2 and then 4 from one table.

Single-pass SCA is the fast mode: all 28 steps at the target, the global view alone for the first 8 as in AReA, then windows of 128, 112, 96, 80 tokens before the native 64. The opening, the window sizes and the bridge are set by hand; only the weights are fitted.

Progressive SCA, 57 steps

Single-pass SCA, 28 steps at 4096²

The 2048² stage re-enters at σ = 0.81, the 4096² stage at σ = 0.61; guidance is 3.5, 4.5 and 6.0. Both upper stages start after the point where single-pass SCA opens with the global view alone, so the 1024² stage takes the place of that opening.

Against HRDiT, the same schedule

No resolved difference in ImageReward (−0.02 [−0.09, 0.03]), HPSv2.1 (+0.10 [−0.14, 0.35], ×100) or PickScore (−0.05 [−0.14, 0.04]), in 49.2 against 107.0 s. Intervals that include zero do not establish equivalence. HRDiT keeps lower KID and patch FID (+4.6 [0.6, 7.7] for progressive SCA).

Against single-pass SCA

Progressive SCA gains ImageReward (+0.07 [0.01, 0.13]) and PickScore (+0.29 [0.20, 0.38]). Single-pass SCA keeps more fine detail: MUSIQ on 1024² crops is −6.0 [−7.3, −4.6] for progressive SCA and its patch FID is higher by +9.2 [5.1, 13.2].

Set-level scores against AReA

FID is unresolved at seed 0 (−3.9 [−8.5, 3.1]). The patch FID gap is significant at seeds 0 and 2 (+4.8 [0.1, 10.6] and +5.4 [0.8, 10.8]) but not at seed 1 (+1.5 [−2.7, 6.9]).

Other baselines

Better than DyPE, SEGA and full attention on 10, 10 and 10 of 10 per-image metrics, and than UltraImage on 9; aesthetic score is unresolved there.

Single pass, more prompts and checkpoints

On HRGen-1K single-pass SCA beats AReA on 10 of 10 per-image metrics. On FLUX.1-Krea-dev it wins 7 of 10 and loses none at 3.1× AReA's speed; ImageReward is unresolved there (+0.020 [−0.020, 0.063]).

FLUX.2-dev

SCA without the window schedule beats full attention on 9 of 10 per-image metrics at 2.0× its speed; ImageReward rises from 0.75 to 1.20 (50 prompts).

Limitations

What SCA does not fix

  • Fine detail. Progressive SCA trades fine detail for preference against single-pass SCA (MUSIQ on 1024² crops −6.0, patch FID +9.2). Its patch FID and patch KID are worse than HRDiT's, and its patch FID is higher than AReA's at two of three seeds.
  • Hand-set parts. The single-pass opening, the window sizes and the bridge are set by hand, because output error cannot see layout. The window sizes were chosen on the 46-prompt ablation subset, which is part of the evaluation set.
  • Dependence on HRDiT. Progressive SCA uses HRDiT's schedule, and that sampler does not run FLUX.1-Krea-dev.
  • The fit. The reach table is fitted without the bridge and with 16 times fewer keys per branch than the target; the error of the deployed operator is not measured.
  • Metrics. All metrics are automatic and the intervals ignore multiple comparisons. FID at 299² sees little of the 4K detail.
  • Other sizes. At 2048 × 4096 single-pass SCA loses 3 metrics to AReA. At 8192² grid and stripe artifacts remain in both SCA and AReA. Faint tiled-VAE seams can show at 1:1 in some images.
