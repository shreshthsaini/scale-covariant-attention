The loop: three 4096² progressive SCA images zoomed to native pixels, the first three of the seeded draw behind the paper’s Figure 1 (one showcase prompt per category).

A 4096² image in 49.2 seconds

SCA replaces only the attention of FLUX.1-dev inside HRDiT’s sampler: 4.1× faster than AReA and better on all ten per-image metrics at three seeds. Against HRDiT it is statistically indistinguishable on three preference scores, in 46% of its time.

How it works

No training and no new model: SCA changes where each attention head looks.

  1. 1

    Some heads look near, some look far, and each keeps its habit through the whole run.

    97.5% of how far a head looks is set by the head, not by the denoising step.

  2. 2

    At any size, every head gets both views: a native-size window around the patch and a native-size grid over the whole image.

  3. 3

    Each head mixes the two by how far it looked at 1024², measured once, with no training.

Where it falls short. Patch FID is higher than HRDiT’s and single-pass SCA’s; the opening, window sizes and bridge are set by hand; at 6144² and 8192² samples show block and stripe artifacts. In single-pass mode, small additional figures occasionally appear in scenes with people or animals; the progressive configuration reduces this to the level of the multi-stage baselines.
